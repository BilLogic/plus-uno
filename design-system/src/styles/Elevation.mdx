<FoundationSection title="Overview">

Elevation is how far a surface sits above the page. Each level is a pair of tokens, a surface color and its shadow, and the two always travel together. A raised card or a menu keeps the page color and lifts by its shadow; only the sunken frame changes fill. In dark, raised and overlay surfaces get lighter as they rise, because a shadow is hard to see on a dark page.

```css
background: var(--ds-surface-raised);
box-shadow: var(--ds-shadow-raised);
```

Figma holds `elevation/surface/*` in `color / foundations` and the `elevation/shadow/*` effect styles. Code reads them as `--ds-surface-*` with `--ds-shadow-*`.

<Canvas of={ElevationStories.Overview} sourceState="none" />

</FoundationSection>

<FoundationSection title="About elevation">

Elevations are the layered surfaces that form the foundation of the UI. They are the blank canvas where text, icons, backgrounds and borders sit. Most elevations are a surface and a shadow; together they give the impression of lift or depth, and they guide focus through layering or show that a surface can be scrolled, slid or dragged.

Elevations use design tokens to apply the surface levels. The highest two, raised and overlay, are paired with shadows to create more depth.

In dark mode a shadow is harder to see, so the surfaces change too: imagine them lit from the front, so the higher the elevation, the lighter the surface. Raised and overlay keep their shadows in dark mode for depth and consistency.

</FoundationSection>

<FoundationSection title="Cards">

A card rests on the raised pair, `--ds-surface-raised` with
`--ds-shadow-raised`, and has no border. The raised shadow is a 1px ring at 5%
with a faint blur, so a resting card still reads almost flat in light. In dark,
the ring is light, so the card keeps an edge.

A clickable card lifts to `--ds-shadow-overlay` on hover and while it is
dragged. Pressed, it settles back to `--ds-shadow-raised` on
`--ds-surface-raised-pressed`. A card that cannot be clicked never lifts.

<FoundationSection title="Choosing a surface">

- Sunken is the lowest level, a backdrop or well where other content sits, such as a column on a board. Use it only on the default surface, never on raised or overlay; to separate other areas, use whitespace or a border.
- `elevation/surface/sunken` is opaque and darkens in both light and dark. `color/background/neutral` is transparent: it darkens in light and lightens in dark, so it adapts to whatever elevation it sits on. Use sunken to group content on the default surface and neutral when the fill must follow the elevation.
- Default is the baseline, a flat surface with no lift. Pair it with a border to make a flat card.
- Elevation surfaces use neutral palettes. When a different color is needed, swap the surface for a solid `color/background/*` token and follow that token's hovered and pressed behavior.

<FoundationSection title="Interaction states and scrolling">

Elevations use surface color changes for hovered and pressed. Use the hovered and pressed tokens of the level the surface is on.

<RefTable
    head={['Level', 'Background', 'Hovered', 'Pressed', 'Shadow']}
    rows={[
        ['Default', <C>elevation/surface</C>, <C>elevation/surface/hovered</C>, <C>elevation/surface/pressed</C>, 'none (add color/border/default for a flat card)'],
        ['Raised', <C>elevation/surface/raised/default</C>, <C>elevation/surface/raised/hovered</C>, <C>elevation/surface/raised/pressed</C>, <C>elevation/shadow/raised</C>],
        ['Overlay', <C>elevation/surface/overlay/default</C>, <C>elevation/surface/overlay/hovered</C>, <C>elevation/surface/overlay/pressed</C>, <C>elevation/shadow/overlay</C>],
    ]}
/>

- Hovered and pressed can instead be shown by moving between elevations, only for default and raised surfaces and never for overlays. Use it sparingly and not on very small UI, where a color change reads better than a lift.
- Use the hovered and pressed tokens or an elevation transition, not both.
- Dragged: a surface being dragged takes the overlay elevation and returns to its own level when dropped.
- Scrolled: use a border at the cut-off first, as in modal sticky headers and footers and the navigation. Use the overflow shadow only where a border could be missed, such as very small UI or tables with borders between cells. Overflow is a shadow only: it sits on whatever surface is beneath it, and there is no overflow surface variable, by design.

<FoundationSubsection title="Layering stack">

Bottom to top: the Page, a sticky header, a Popup, the Blanket with its Modal, then a Flag. Each layer sits over the one before it.

1. **Page.** No z-index of its own; the base every other layer sits on.
2. **Sticky header.** Shares the card index. Stays at the top of the Page while content scrolls.
3. **Popup.** Menus and popups open over the header and the Page.
4. **Blanket and Modal.** The Blanket dims everything below and the Modal sits on it.
5. **Flag.** A Flag stays visible above the Modal, so a message is never hidden.

</FoundationSubsection>

<FoundationSection title="Tooltip">

A tooltip is the one floating layer that is not on the overlay pair. It reads as a label, so it takes the dark neutral fill and the inverse text.

- Menus, popups, flags and the floating sidebar take the overlay surface and shadow. A tooltip does not.

<FoundationSection title="Do and don't">

How to lift a surface, and what not to do.

**Lift with shadow, not with a darker fill.** A raised card keeps the page color and takes its shadow. A gray fill reads as a recessed area, so it makes a card look pressed in.

<Canvas of={ElevationStories.LiftWithShadow} sourceState="none" />

**No border on a card.** The ring in the raised shadow is the edge. Adding a border doubles it and makes the card look outlined rather than lifted.

<Canvas of={ElevationStories.NoCardBorder} sourceState="none" />

**Name the shadow token.** A shadow is always one of the `--ds-shadow-*` tokens, never a literal value, so it follows the color mode and the contrast setting.

**Always pair the surface with its shadow.** A surface and its shadow travel together. A menu on the overlay surface with no shadow melts into the page behind it.

<Canvas of={ElevationStories.AlwaysPair} sourceState="none" />

**Raise one focal point.** Use raised intentionally. If it is for emphasis, limit it to one section or focal point of the screen; a border or whitespace is enough to group the rest.

<DoDont>
<Do caption="One raised card marks the next session.">
<div style={{ display: 'flex', flexDirection: 'column', gap: 'var(--stack-md)' }}>
    <Kard value="Next session" />

    <div style={{ padding: 'var(--card-pad-compact)', borderRadius: 'var(--card-radius)', boxShadow: 'inset 0 0 0 var(--border-default) var(--ds-border)', font: 'var(--body-small)' }}>Past session</div>

</Dont>
</DoDont>

**Limit overlays.** When controls must sit over other UI, group related actions in one floating toolbar rather than several overlay buttons. One overlay toolbar holds the related actions; separate floating buttons side by side pile up shadows.

</FoundationSection>

<FoundationSection title="Accessibility">

- A shadow is never the only thing that tells two surfaces apart. Content on a card or a menu still has its own heading, and a modal sits on a Blanket.
- Under increased contrast, the ring in the raised and overlay shadows darkens to a 3:1 line, so cards and menus keep a visible edge. In light increased contrast the frame turns white, and the Page draws a border so its edge stays.
- Text on any of the five surfaces uses the text tokens from Color, which clear 4.5:1 on all of them.
- Check contrast on overlay surfaces in dark mode: raised and overlay surfaces get lighter, so text and icons that pass in light mode may need a different token there.

</FoundationSection>
