<FoundationSection title="Overview">

Every color is a semantic token named for the job it does: page text, field edge, or primary button fill. Each token resolves per mode, so components adapt to light, dark and increased contrast automatically.

```css
color: var(--ds-text-danger);
background: var(--ds-background-danger-subtler);
```

Figma holds them in `color / semantics` (modes Light, Dark, Light IC and Dark IC); `color / primitives` stay hidden. See <DocsLink id="foundations-tokens-color">Tokens: Color</DocsLink> for the full collection.

<Canvas of={ColorsStories.Overview} sourceState="none" />

</FoundationSection>

<FoundationSection title="How the tokens are built">

Primitives hold raw ramps and stay hidden; bind only semantic tokens. Names read from general to specific: property, role, emphasis, state. In Figma, parts join with slashes (`color/background/danger/bold/hovered`); in CSS, drop `color` and join with dashes behind `--ds-` (`--ds-background-danger-bold-hovered`).

<FoundationSection title="Modes">

PLUS ships four modes: light and dark, each at standard and increased contrast. `auto` follows OS `prefers-color-scheme` and `prefers-contrast`.

- **Dark mode** is not light inverted: raised and overlay surfaces step lighter as they rise, because shadows are subtle on dark grounds. Design in dark mode by choosing the same token; never map your own values.
- **Increased contrast** pushes text to 7:1 and borders, icons and the focus ring to 4.5:1. Tints, surfaces and the Blanket stay unchanged.
- **In code**, set `data-color-mode` (`light`, `dark`, `auto`) and `data-contrast-mode` (`no-preference`, `more`, `auto`) on `<html>`. No attribute means light at standard contrast.
- **In Figma**, set the frame mode to Light, Dark, Light IC or Dark IC.

</FoundationSection>

<FoundationSection title="States">

Hover and press step along the token's own ladder. Focus is always a ring, never a fill (<DocsLink id="foundations-visual-border">Border</DocsLink>). AI color tokens live on <DocsLink id="ai-ui-foundations-color">AI UI › Color</DocsLink>.

- **Rows, menu items and sidebar items** take `color/background/neutral/subtle/hovered` and `/pressed`.
- **Selected elements** take `color/background/selected` (`selected/hovered` and `/pressed` on interaction), paired with a second cue such as a check mark or border. Never use brand, link or focused tokens for selection. Keep selected and focused distinct: selected persists to show a current choice, focus shows where the keyboard is, and a selected item stays recognizable while hovered or pressed.
- **Fields** take `color/background/input/hovered` with `color/border/input` on hover; on focus they keep their fill and add the focus ring.
- **Icons** have no hovered or pressed color tokens; show interaction with a subtle neutral background behind the icon.
- **Avatars and images** whose fill cannot change use `color/interaction/hovered` and `color/interaction/pressed` overlays.

```css
.my-element:hover {
  background-color: var(--ds-background-neutral-subtle-hovered);
}
.my-element:active {
  background-color: var(--ds-background-neutral-subtle-pressed);
}
.my-element.is-selected {
  background-color: var(--ds-background-selected);
}
```

</FoundationSection>

<FoundationSection title="Partner pairs and contrast">

Every foreground token pairs with a ground that clears WCAG AA across all four modes: 4.5:1 for text (7:1 in IC) and 3:1 for icons, borders and the focus ring (4.5:1 in IC).

- **Bold fills** take `color/text/inverse` and `color/icon/inverse`, except bold warning which takes dark `color/text/warning/inverse`.
- **Status tints** take the role's text token, or its `-bolder` step on `-subtler` and `-subtle` grounds.
- **Surfaces** take `color/text/default`, `subtle` or `subtlest`.
- Never carry meaning by color alone: a status has an icon or a word, and a chart has a legend or direct labels. Use 3:1 for UI essential to understanding.

<FoundationSection title="Data-viz color">

Charts use `--chart-*` tokens (`chartPalette` from `@/components`). Never reach for an accent hue or a UI role token to draw a series. Color is the one foundation with chart rules of its own; charts take spacing, radius, elevation, stroke, type, layers and motion from the same component tokens as the rest of the system.

- Use five or six categories at most and group the rest into "Other".
- Never mix categorical and status colors in one chart, and give every series or state a second cue (label, shape, pattern, icon or backing table).
- Titles, axes, gridlines, tooltips and threshold lines wear text and border tokens, never a chart color.

<FoundationSubsection title="Categorical order">

Assign series in slot order without skipping.

<RefTable
    head={['Slot', 'Hue', 'Light', 'Dark', 'Light IC', 'Dark IC']}
    rows={[
        ['1 (brand)', 'lagoon', '600', '500', '850', '300'],
        ['2', 'orange', '800', '700', '1000', '500'],
        ['3', 'blue', '500', '500', '800', '300'],
        ['4', 'magenta', '800', '700', '1000', '500'],
        ['5', 'lime', '500', '400', '800', '250'],
        ['6', 'purple', '700', '700', '900', '500'],
        ['7', 'orange', '500', '500', '800', '300'],
        ['8', 'blue', '800', '700', '1000', '500'],
    ]}
/>

Three rules govern this sequence:

- **Brand leads:** slot 1 is lagoon (`--chart-brand`), so a one-series chart and the first series of a multi-series chart share their lead color.
- **Neighbors alternate light and dark steps:** slot 2 (orange-800) sits between slot 1 (lagoon-600) and slot 3 (blue-500), alternating through slot 6 so adjacent series remain distinct in grayscale and under color-vision deficiency.
- **Hues spread across the wheel:** lagoon, orange, blue, magenta, lime and purple never place two adjacent slots in the same hue family. Yellow (warning), red (danger) and teal (too close to brand) stay out; lime and purple-500 double as success and discovery, which is why categorical and status colors never mix.
- **Overflow and dark mode:** slots 7 and 8 reprise orange and blue for overflow past six series. Dark mode steps one lighter except blue (3) and orange (7), which hold at 500 to preserve lightness alternation, and purple (6), which holds at 700 so it does not crowd its neighbors.

</FoundationSubsection>

<FoundationSubsection title="Separators, ramps, SMART subjects and hover">

- **Separators:** keep a gap or `--ds-border-inverse` line where marks touch (stacked bars, histogram bins, waterfall steps). Lightness alternation identifies the series; the separator marks the boundary.
- **Sequential and diverging ramps:** sequential runs `--ds-background-accent-<hue>-subtler` through `--chart-<hue>-bold`, `-bolder` and `-boldest` (strongest tone = largest value). Diverging runs two hues around `--ds-background-accent-gray-subtler` (orange and blue by default; never red and green).
- **SMART subjects:** each subject uses the emphasis that keeps S-M-A-R-T neighbors apart: social-emotional orange (`bold`), mastering content teal (`boldest`), advocacy lime (`bold`), relationship magenta (`boldest`), technology tools brown (`bold`). Label directly or keep the legend beside the chart.
- **Labels and hover:** place labels beside marks; when a label must sit on a fill (heatmap cell, stacked segment), use `--ds-text` or `--ds-text-inverse`. Hovered marks take their `-hovered` token, and unselected series fade with `--ds-opacity-disabled`.

</FoundationSubsection>

</FoundationSection>

<FoundationSection title="Accessibility">

- Foreground/background partner pairs clear WCAG 2.1 AA in all four modes: 4.5:1 for text smaller than 24 px (1.4.3) and 3:1 for essential UI, icons, borders, the focus ring and text 24 px or larger (1.4.11), rising to 7:1 and 4.5:1 in increased contrast.
- Never convey meaning by color alone: pair status colors with an icon or text, selection with a check mark or 2 px border, and chart series with labels, shapes or a table.
- Warning icons and borders use `color/icon/warning` and `color/border/warning` rather than the yellow fill (2.87:1 on the darkest surface) so they clear 3:1.
- Check new screens in all four modes with the toolbar's Color mode and Contrast switches, and see <DocsLink id="foundations-accessibility">Accessibility</DocsLink> for cross-cutting rules.
