## Usage

Use `SplitButton` when one action is clearly primary and a few related
actions belong beside it in a menu — "Send" with "Send and close," "Save" as
a draft. For actions with no clear primary, use `ButtonGroup` instead.

</div>

<div className="sb-ds-doc-section">

## Parts

1. **Action.** A Button for the primary action.
2. **Seam.** The two children meet at a flat edge: inner corners are square, outer corners keep the button radius.
3. **Menu trigger.** An Icon button with a chevron that opens the related actions.

## Best practices

### Keep the two halves joined.

The primary action and the menu trigger share one outline and one height.

<DoDontPair
    doExample={<SplitButton><Button appearance="primary" text="Save" /><IconButton appearance="primary" icon="chevron-down" label="More save options" /></SplitButton>}
    doCaption="One outline around both halves."
    dontExample={<div style={{ display: 'flex', gap: 'var(--inline-md)' }}><Button appearance="primary" text="Save" /><IconButton appearance="primary" icon="chevron-down" label="More save options" /></div>}
    dontCaption="Two separate buttons with a gap."
/>

### The menu holds related actions only.

Every menu item is a variation of the primary action: "Save", then "Save as draft" and "Save as template". "Delete account" or "Invite tutor" belong somewhere else.

### Put the common action on the face.

The half people use most sits on the face; the rarer ones go in the menu.

<DoDontPair
    doExample={<SplitButton><Button appearance="primary" text="Save" /><IconButton appearance="primary" icon="chevron-down" label="More save options" /></SplitButton>}
    doCaption={'"Save" on the face.'}
    dontExample={<SplitButton><Button appearance="primary" text="Save as template" /><IconButton appearance="primary" icon="chevron-down" label="More save options" /></SplitButton>}
    dontCaption={'The rarely used "Save as template" on the face.'}
/>

### Match appearance and spacing on both children yourself.

`SplitButton` draws the seam but sets no styling of its own. Pass the same
`appearance` and `spacing` to the `Button` and the chevron `IconButton` so
the control reads as one piece.

<DoDontPair
    doExample={<SplitButton><Button appearance="primary" text="Send" /><IconButton appearance="primary" icon="chevron-down" label="More send options" /></SplitButton>}
    doCaption="Both halves use the primary appearance."
    dontExample={<SplitButton><Button text="Send" /><IconButton appearance="primary" icon="chevron-down" label="More send options" /></SplitButton>}
    dontCaption="The two halves disagree on appearance, so they read as two controls."
/>

```jsx
<SplitButton>
    <Button text="Send" onClick={send} appearance="primary" />
    <IconButton icon="chevron-down" label="More send options" appearance="primary" onClick={openMenu} />
</SplitButton>
```

### Open the menu from the chevron's own click handler.

`SplitButton` has no menu logic itself — wire the chevron's `onClick` to open
a `Popup` or `MenuGroup` positioned under it.

## Content guidelines

<DocsTable>
<thead>
<tr><th>Do</th><th>Don't</th></tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td>Label the main action with a verb and its object: "Publish page".</td><td>A bare "Go" or "More".</td></tr>

<tr><td>Use sentence case on both halves.</td><td>Title case or all capitals.</td></tr>
<tr><td>List the alternatives in the menu in the same voice as the main action: "Publish and notify".</td><td>Menu items that are not variations of the main action.</td></tr>
</tbody>

## Accessibility

- **A group of two controls.** The wrapper is `role="group"`, so
  assistive technology reads the action and its menu trigger as related. It
  has no name of its own; pass `aria-label` to give it one.
- **Each child keeps its own accessible name.** The primary action needs its
  own label; the chevron's `IconButton` needs a label describing what its
  menu holds, not a generic "more."
