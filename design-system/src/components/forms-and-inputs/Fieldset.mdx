## Usage

Use `Fieldset` when a set of mixed controls shares one legend and one validation message, such as related text fields asked together.

</div>

<div className="sb-ds-doc-section">

## Alternatives

- For a group of radio buttons under one question, use `RadioGroup`.
- For a group of checkboxes under one question, use `CheckboxGroup`.
- For a single field with its own label and caption, use `TextField` or `Select`.

</div>

<div className="sb-ds-doc-section">

## Parts

1. **Legend.** Names the group, in body default. A required marker follows it.
2. **Fields.** The grouped fields, stacked with `--stack-md` between them.
3. **Error.** One message under the fields, in body small.

## Best practices

### One `error` covers the whole group.

Pass `error` on the Fieldset, not on each field inside it, when the validation applies to the set as a whole: "Choose at least one", for example, rather than a field-by-field message.

```jsx
<Fieldset legend="Notify me by" isRequired error={error}>
    <Checkbox label="Email" />
    <Checkbox label="Text" />
</Fieldset>
```

### Build on a fieldset rather than reimplementing it.

`RadioGroup` and `CheckboxGroup` already compose `Fieldset` with `Radio` and `Checkbox` children. Reach for those directly for single-question choice sets, and use `Fieldset` on its own when grouping custom or mixed fields.

<DoDontPair
    doExample={<Fieldset legend="Notify me by"><Checkbox label="Email" /><Checkbox label="Text" /></Fieldset>}
    doCaption="A fieldset with one legend for the set."
    dontExample={<div><Checkbox label="Email" /><Checkbox label="Text" /></div>}
    dontCaption="Loose boxes in a plain container, with no legend."
/>

### Group related fields under one legend.

The legend names what the fields have in common. Without it a screen reader hears loose, unrelated fields.

<DoDontPair
    doExample={<Fieldset legend="Contact details"><TextField label="Email" /><TextField label="Phone" /></Fieldset>}
    doCaption="The legend names the group."
    dontExample={<Fieldset legend="Fields"><TextField label="Email" /><TextField label="Phone" /></Fieldset>}
    dontCaption="A legend that says nothing."
/>

### Keep the legend to a few words.

A legend is a label, not an instruction. Two or three words stay on one line.

<DoDontPair
    doExample={<Fieldset legend="Contact"><TextField label="Email" /><TextField label="Phone" /></Fieldset>}
    doCaption="One short line."
    dontExample={<Fieldset legend="Please fill in your contact details in the fields below"><TextField label="Email" /><TextField label="Phone" /></Fieldset>}
    dontCaption="A whole sentence as the legend."
/>

### Name the group, not the action.

The legend says what the fields are. The button says what to do with them.

<DoDontPair
    doExample={<Fieldset legend="Contact details"><TextField label="Email" /><TextField label="Phone" /></Fieldset>}
    doCaption="A noun phrase."
    dontExample={<Fieldset legend="Fill in below"><TextField label="Email" /><TextField label="Phone" /></Fieldset>}
    dontCaption="An instruction."
/>

## Accessibility

- **A real `fieldset` and `legend` pair.** The legend is tied to the group through native HTML semantics, which every screen reader already understands.
- **The error is announced once.** `role="alert"` on the error message means assistive technology hears it as soon as it appears, without needing focus to move there.
- **`isRequired` is visual and spoken.** The asterisk is `aria-hidden`, and a visually hidden "(required)" is added to the legend's accessible name.
