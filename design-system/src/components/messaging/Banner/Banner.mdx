## Usage

Use `Banner` for a condition that applies to the whole page, for as long as it
holds: scheduled maintenance, an outage, an account-wide notice. It has no
timeout and no dismiss, so only mount it while the condition is true.

For something that belongs to one part of the page and stays until resolved
or dismissed, use `SectionMessage`. For a confirmation that something already
happened and needs no reply, use `Flag` — a `normal`, `information` or `success` flag times out
on its own, and `warning` or `error` stays until dismissed.

</div>

1. **Icon.** Leading glyph for <code>warning</code> and <code>danger</code>; <code>announcement</code> has none.

## Best practices

### Pick the appearance for what is true, not for emphasis.

`warning` is the default, cautionary form. `danger` is for something that
needs immediate attention. `announcement` carries no icon and no severity —
use it for a general notice with nothing wrong.

### Keep the message to one line where it can fit.

A banner runs the full width of the page, so a short message reads at a
glance. A link inside the message inherits the banner's text color and is
underlined to stay visible against every fill.

```jsx
<Banner appearance="danger">
    The scheduled call is delayed. <a href="/status">Check status</a>.
</Banner>
```

## Accessibility

- **Announced as a status.** The banner's role is `status`, so assistive
  technology reports its content without interrupting the current task.
- **Color is not the only cue.** `warning` and `danger` pair their fill with a
  leading icon; only `announcement` relies on fill alone, which is
  appropriate since it carries no severity.
- **No focus trap.** A banner never captures focus or keyboard navigation —
  it sits in the page's reading order like any other content.
- **Severity needs more than color.** Give the warning and danger icons their meaning in the words of the message, since the glyph is decorative.
- **A banner link takes focus like any link.** It shows the 2px focus ring and its text names the destination.
