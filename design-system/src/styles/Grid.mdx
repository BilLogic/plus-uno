PLUS lays pages out on two 12-column grids with a 16 gutter at every width,
and page content caps at 1232. The grid decides where containers sit: cards,
tables, forms and text blocks. The [spacing tokens](?path=/docs/foundations-spacing--docs)
decide everything inside them. PLUS is desktop only, from 768 up.

### Overview

<Canvas of={GridStories.GridAnatomy} sourceState="none" />

In code, a region lays itself out on the grid rather than reading column
widths:

```css
display: grid;
grid-template-columns: repeat(var(--layout-columns), minmax(0, 1fr));
gap: var(--layout-gutter);
```

### Breakpoints

Each breakpoint is one minimum width and one mode. The column count stays 12
and the gutter 16 at every width. The sidebar docks from 1024.

<RefTable
    head={['Mode', 'From', 'Code', 'Sidebar', 'Surface pad', 'Content width', 'Column']}
    rows={[
        ['Medium', '768', <C>--breakpoint-md-min</C>, 'closed', '16', '720', '45.33'],
        ['Large', '1024', <C>--breakpoint-lg-min</C>, 'docked, 240', '16', '744', '47.33'],
        ['X-Large', '1440', <C>--breakpoint-xl-min</C>, 'docked, 240', '24', '1144', '80.67'],
    ]}
/>

Code also defines `--breakpoint-xxl-min` (1920), but nothing switches there.
Below 768 nothing switches either: the content holds its 720 minimum and the
surface scrolls sideways. Design at Medium and X-Large at least, so the stretch
between them is intentional.

In Figma, a breakpoint is a mode of the `spacing / layout` collection. A frame
changes breakpoint by switching its mode, never by hand-editing its grid.
</div>

### Two grids

Both grids are 12 columns with a 16 gutter.

- **The screen grid** spans the screen inside the screen pad, centered and
  capped at 1232. It ignores the sidebar, so an overlay sits in the same place
  whether the sidebar is docked or closed. Modals and overlays size from it.
  In Figma it lives on the Overlay layer only, and shows and hides with it.
- **The content grid** sits inside the surface, after the sidebar and the
  surface pad take their share. Cards, tables, forms and text blocks align to
  it, and it is the only grid a page frame carries. At X-Large:
  1440 − 240 sidebar − 8 container pad − 2 × 24 = 1144.

These tokens change with the screen, so the table gives the value at each
breakpoint: Medium · Large · X-Large.

### The frame

The frame (Surface container in Figma, `Frame` in code) draws the shell the
content grid is measured in: the topbar, then a row with the sidebar and the
surface.

<TokenTable
    valueHeader="MD · LG · XL"

        { token: '--layout-width-sidebar', figma: 'Sidebar/width-sidebar', value: '240 · 240 · 240' },
        { token: '--layout-pad-left-container', figma: 'Sidebar/pad-left-container', value: '8 · 0 · 0' },
        { token: '--surface-pad', figma: 'Surface/pad', value: '16 · 16 · 24' },

The container pad and stack are 8. The topbar is 32 with no vertical pad. Below
1024 the sidebar opens over the page: 240 wide, full height, with a shadow and
no scrim. An outside click closes it, and the page never reflows. The dock is a
step: at 1023 the content is 975 wide, and at 1024 the sidebar docks and it is
744, so cards reflow there while modals keep their width.

### Column spans

A region takes whole columns: span n = n × column + (n − 1) × 16.

<Canvas of={GridStories.ColumnSpans} sourceState="none" />

Cards per row is the most of 4, 3, 2 or 1 that keeps each card at its minimum
width: `--card-min-width-sm` 224, `-md` 304, `-lg` 480. In a section narrower
than 12 columns, only counts that divide its span apply. A split section keeps
its main column at least 480 (`--section-min-width-main`) and its side column
at least 224 (`--section-min-width-side`); below those, they stack.
</div>

### Modal widths

A modal spans 6, 8 or 10 screen-grid columns, the gutters between them, and its
own pad of 16 on each side: width = n × column + (n − 1) × 16 + 2 × 16. It
grows with the screen between breakpoints, stops past a 1280 screen, never
shrinks below its Medium width, ignores the sidebar, and never comes closer
than 16 to the side of the screen.

### Usage

The grid owns where top-level containers sit; spacing owns everything inside
them.

**Keep the gutter 16 at every width.** Columns flex with the screen; the
gutter never changes.

<Canvas of={GridStories.Gutter} sourceState="none" />

**Span whole columns.** A content container spans whole columns (3, 4, 6 or
12) and can be centered by leaving the same number of columns empty on each
side. Content never bleeds into a gutter or into the surface pad.

<Canvas of={GridStories.SpanWholeColumns} sourceState="none" />

**Size modals from the screen grid.** Pass `sm`, `md` or `lg`. The width comes
from screen-grid columns, so a modal lines up with the page whenever the
sidebar is closed.

<Canvas of={GridStories.ModalFromScreenGrid} sourceState="none" />

**Cap reading text at 864.** Long-form text uses the reading cap, so lines stay
readable. Structured content, such as dashboards and tables, uses the 1232 cap.

<Canvas of={GridStories.ReadingCap} sourceState="none" />

**Align containers, not their parts.** Only top-level containers align to
columns: cards, tables, forms, images, charts and text blocks. Inside a
container the grid stops and spacing tokens take over, so buttons, icons, tags
and avatars never stretch to columns. Cards have no inner grid.

<Canvas of={GridStories.AlignContainers} sourceState="none" />

**Change breakpoint by mode.** In Figma, use one page frame and switch its
`spacing / layout` mode: the grid, the widths and the sidebar follow together.
Never keep a copy of the page per breakpoint with hand-edited grids. The copies
drift, and the variable bindings stop working.

**Put the screen grid on the Overlay layer only.** A page frame carries only
the content grid.

**Design from 768 up.** PLUS has no small-screen tier. Never design below 768,
and never drop to 6 or 2 columns: a four-card row at X-Large is still a
four-card row at Medium, just narrower.
</div>

<div className="sb-ds-doc-section">
### Accessibility

- Long-form text stays under the 864 reading cap, so a line is easy to follow
  to its end and back.
- Content never sits under the sidebar or the topbar. Below 1024 the sidebar
  opens over the page and closes on an outside click or Escape, and focus
  returns to its toggle.
- At the 720 minimum the surface scrolls sideways instead of squeezing
  content, so text never overlaps or clips when the page is zoomed.
</div>
