## Anatomy

- **Frame.** A 1000 × 1000 square in Figma, scaled to `size` in code. It is the
  layout box: what sits next to a character lines up with the frame.
- **Body.** The textured shape, in the body token. The stipple texture is part
  of the art, so the body reads as soft at every size.
- **Face, arms and legs.** Line work in `--ds-icon-subtle`. Most characters
  reach past the frame with their limbs.

## Color and the SMART mapping

Every body sits at one mid strength, so no character reads louder than the
others in either mode. The five SMART characters use their area's `subtle`
step, which exists for illustration bodies. The seven math characters use an
accent hue at the same strength, and never a SMART hue, so a math character is
never read as a SMART area. There are only four accent hues left once the SMART
hues are set aside, so three of them repeat.

## Sizes

<DocsCanvasShell description={<>48, 96, 160 and 240 are the named sizes; any number of pixels also works. The size is the frame's width and height, and the limbs reach past it.</>}>
    <DsCanvasQuiet of={IllustrationStories.Sizes} />
</DocsCanvasShell>

- **48, 96, 160, 240.** Below 48 the line work breaks into specks; above 240
  the stipple starts to read as noise.
- **The safe area is outside the frame.** The limbs are not clipped. Across
  the twelve characters they reach up to about 33% of the size to the left,
  60% to the right, 14% above and 50% below; dot reaches farthest. Leave that
  much room on the sides a character has limbs on, or the limbs run into what
  sits next to it.

## Usage

Use `Illustration` to give a page or a moment some warmth: the header of Home,
an empty state, a welcome, or a card that introduces a SMART area. A character
is decoration beside words that already say what the page is about.

Use an icon, not a character, for anything a person acts on or has to read to
understand. Use `Tile` for a small square picture of a thing.

</div>

## Parts

1. **Frame.** The square layout box, `size` wide and high. The limbs reach past it and are never clipped.
2. **Body.** The textured shape, filled from a body token and masked by the stipple.
3. **Line work.** The face, arms and legs, in one icon token.

## Best practices

### Pick a SMART character for a SMART area, and a math character for anything else.

```jsx
// Incorrect: a math character standing in for a SMART area, and a SMART
// character used as generic decoration on a page about something else.
<Illustration character="pi" title="Advocacy" />
<Illustration character="relationships" />  {/* on a billing page */}

// Correct.
<Illustration character="advocacy" title="Advocacy" />
<Illustration character="pi" />
```

### Keep characters decorative unless they carry meaning.

```jsx
// Incorrect: a title on a character beside a heading that already says it.
<h2>Advocacy</h2>
<Illustration character="advocacy" title="Advocacy" />

// Correct: decorative beside the heading; titled when it stands alone.
<h2>Advocacy</h2>
<Illustration character="advocacy" />
```

### Don't recolor or crop a character.

```jsx
// Incorrect: the colors come from tokens, so a style override breaks the
// color modes; and clipping the box cuts the limbs off.
<Illustration character="dot" style={{ filter: 'hue-rotate(90deg)' }} />
<div style={{ overflow: 'hidden' }}><Illustration character="dot" /></div>

// Correct: give it room instead.
<div style={{ padding: 'var(--size-section-gap-lg)' }}><Illustration character="dot" /></div>
```

## Accessibility

- **Decorative by default.** Without a `title` a character is hidden from
  assistive technology (`aria-hidden="true"`), since it sits beside words that
  already say what the page is about.
- **`title` makes it one image with a name.** The character becomes
  `role="img"` with that name, and its parts stay hidden, so it is announced
  once. Give it a title only when it carries meaning on its own.
- **The line ratio is a property of the source art.** The line work measures
  about 2.6:1 on the bodies in Light and about 2.7:1 in Dark. That is under 3:1,
  and it is fine for decoration, which has no contrast requirement. Never put
  information in a character that a person needs to read.
- **Every mode.** Bodies and line work take their colors from tokens alone, so
  a character follows Light, Dark and increased contrast with nothing to set.
