<Title />

Color in PLUS is a set of `--ds-*` tokens, each named for the job it does: the
text on a page, the edge of a field, the fill of the primary button. A token
has one value in each color mode, so a component that uses the right token is
right in light, dark and increased contrast without any work of its own. The
examples on this page draw both modes side by side, and they follow the
toolbar's **Color mode** and **Contrast** switches.

### How the tokens are built

There are two layers.

- **Primitives** are the raw ramps: `lagoon` (the brand), `neutral`, and the
  accent hues blue, teal, green, lime, yellow, orange, red, magenta, purple
  and brown. In CSS they are `--palette-lagoon-700` and so on. They exist so
  the semantic tokens have something to point at. Never use one in a design or
  a component.
- **Semantic tokens** point at a primitive and name a job. They are the only
  color tokens you bind. Their names follow Atlassian's, so an Atlassian
  guideline usually applies to PLUS word for word.

A semantic name reads from the general to the specific:
`property.role.emphasis.state`. In Figma the parts are joined with slashes
(`color/text/subtle`). In CSS, drop the leading `color.` and join the parts
with dashes behind `--ds-` (`--ds-text-subtle`).

while the pointer is over it, and `--ds-text-subtlest` is the faintest text,
used for metadata such as a timestamp.

### Modes

PLUS has two color modes, **light** and **dark**, and each has an
**increased contrast** version for people who need stronger text and edges.
That makes four modes. A fifth setting, **auto**, follows the operating system:
`prefers-color-scheme` picks light or dark, and `prefers-contrast` turns
increased contrast on.

- **Dark** is not light inverted. Raised and overlay surfaces get lighter as
  they rise, because a shadow is hard to see on a dark page.
- **Increased contrast** darkens text and meaningful borders in light and
  lightens them in dark. Text then reaches 7:1, and borders, icons and the
  focus ring reach 4.5:1. Tints, surfaces and the scrim stay as they are.

The mode is set once, on the page, by two attributes: `data-color-mode`
(`light`, `dark` or `auto`) and `data-contrast-mode` (`no-preference`, `more`
or `auto`). No attribute means light at standard contrast. In Storybook, the
toolbar sets both. Each swatch below shows the value the page resolves for its
token right now, so switching the toolbar changes it at once.

### Text and icons

Text comes in three emphasis levels of one neutral: `--ds-text` for body text
and labels, `--ds-text-subtle` for secondary text and helper text, and
`--ds-text-subtlest` for metadata and placeholders. There is no secondary or
tertiary text color. Icons follow the same three levels.

Links take `--ds-link`. Brand text that is not a link takes `--ds-text-brand`.
A status word takes its role's text token, such as `--ds-text-danger`.

### Borders

`--ds-border` is the hairline for dividers and the edges of containers.
`--ds-border-input` is the edge of a field or control, and
`--ds-border-focused` is the focus ring. A border that carries meaning, such
as an invalid field's `--ds-border-danger`, clears 3:1. A `-subtle` border is
decorative and does not have to.

### Backgrounds and surfaces

A **surface** is what content sits on: the page, a card, a menu. A
**background** is the fill of a part: a button, a selected row, a chip.

- `--ds-background-neutral-subtle` is transparent at rest. Menu items and
  subtle buttons use it and take its `-hovered` and `-pressed` steps.
- `--ds-background-brand-bold` is the primary action.
- `--ds-background-selected` marks a selected row or tab, with
  `--ds-text-selected` on it. `--ds-background-selected-bold` is a checked
  checkbox or a selected radio, never a primary action.
- `--ds-blanket` is the scrim under a modal.

### Status colors

Danger, warning, success, information and discovery each come in four
emphasis levels:

- the **default** tint, for a section message;
- **subtler**, for a status label;
- **subtle**, for a chip that is not interactive;
- **bold**, the filled version, with `--ds-text-inverse` on it.

Warning's bold fill is a light yellow, so the text on it is
`--ds-text-warning-inverse`, the dark ink. Discovery marks something new or
proposed, such as a Suggestion.

### Choosing a token

1. **Name the property.** Is the color painting text, an icon, a border or a
   background? That is the first part of the name.
2. **Name the role.** Default neutral, brand, selected, a status, or an
   accent. If the color means nothing in particular, it is neutral.
3. **Pick the emphasis.** Start at the default. Step to `subtle` or `bold` only
   when the design calls for less or more weight.
4. **Add the state** if the element is interactive: `-hovered`, `-pressed`.

Pick by meaning, never by a matching value. A danger fill, its border and its
icon are three tokens even when two of them share a value today. When the
values change, a borrowed token will not follow.

Interaction states use each role's own steps. A row or menu item takes
`--ds-background-neutral-subtle-hovered` and `-pressed`, and a bold fill takes
its own `-hovered` and `-pressed`. Only an avatar or an image, whose fill
cannot change, lays a translucent `--ds-interaction-hovered` over itself.
Focus is never a fill: it is the 2px focus ring in `--ds-border-focused`.

### Partner pairs and contrast

Every foreground token has a background it is made for, and the pair clears
the WCAG bar in all four modes: 4.5:1 for text, 3:1 for icons, borders and the
focus ring, and 7:1 and 4.5:1 under increased contrast.

- **On a bold fill**, text and icons take `--ds-text-inverse` and
  `--ds-icon-inverse`. Warning is the exception: `--ds-text-warning-inverse`.
- **On a status tint**, text takes the role's text token
  (`--ds-text-danger` on `--ds-background-danger`), or its `-bolder` step on
  `-subtler` and `-subtle`.
- **On any surface**, text takes `--ds-text`, `--ds-text-subtle` or
  `--ds-text-subtlest`. All three keep 4.5:1 on hovered and selected rows too.

Each ratio below is measured from the rendered colors.

### SMART subjects

The five SMART areas of tutoring each have a role of their own:
`--ds-background-smart-<subject>` for a chip, with `--ds-text-smart-<subject>`
and `--ds-border-smart-<subject>`; `-bold` for a filled mark; and `-subtle`, a
middle step made for illustration bodies. The subjects are
`social-emotional`, `mastering-content`, `advocacy`, `relationship` and
`technology-tools`. Use a SMART color only to name its subject.

### Data-viz color

Charts take the `--ds-chart-*` tokens, and every chart color does one job.

<RefTable
    head={['Job', 'What it encodes', 'Tokens']}
    rows={[
        ['One series', 'The data, with nothing to tell apart', <><C>--ds-chart-brand</C>; a comparison or reference line takes <C>--ds-chart-neutral</C></>],
        ['Categorical', 'Which series', <><C>--ds-chart-categorical-1</C> to <C>-6</C>, in order</>],
        ['Sequential', 'How much', 'One hue, from its subtlest step to its boldest; the strongest tone is the biggest value'],
        ['Diverging', 'Which side of a baseline', 'Two hues around a gray midpoint, orange and blue by default'],
        ['Status', 'On track, at risk, off track', <><C>--ds-chart-success</C>, <C>-warning</C>, <C>-danger</C>, each with <C>-bold</C></>],
        ['SMART subject', 'Which subject', <C>--ds-chart-smart-&lt;subject&gt;</C>],
    ]}
/>

Use five or six categories at most and group the rest. Never mix categorical
and status colors in one chart, and give every color a second cue: a label, a
shape or a pattern. Titles, axes and grid lines wear text and border tokens,
never a chart color. The full rules, with the categorical order and the ramps,
are in the [data-viz color guide](https://github.com/BilLogic/plus-uno/blob/main/design-system/guidelines/foundations/color.md#data-viz-color),
and every chart under **Data visualizations** already applies them.
</div>

### Legacy color names

The older names (`--color-primary`, `--color-on-surface`,
`--color-surface-container-*`, `--elevation-light-*`) still resolve, so
components that have not moved yet keep rendering. Each now points at a
`--ds-*` token where one meaning fits. Don't use them in new work.

### Accessibility

- Text clears 4.5:1 and icons, borders and the focus ring 3:1 against what
  they sit on, in light and dark. Under increased contrast the bars are 7:1
  and 4.5:1. Use the partner pairs above and the bar is met.
- Never carry meaning by color alone. A status has an icon or a word, a chart
  has a legend or direct labels, and a selected row has more than a tint.
- Check a new screen in all four modes before review: the toolbar's
  **Color mode** and **Contrast** switches set them here.
</div>
