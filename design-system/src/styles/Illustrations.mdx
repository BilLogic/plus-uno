<FoundationSection title="Overview">

In PLUS the illustrations are hand-drawn characters: one for each SMART area, plus a set of math-symbol characters. Use one where a page has room to breathe, such as an empty state, onboarding or a moment of completion. Its body takes the area's color token, so the character follows Light and Dark.

The characters are spot illustrations. A low-fidelity illustration of the UI uses basic shapes in mostly gray and never a real screenshot. The component page lives under Components, Images and icons.

</FoundationSection>

<FoundationSection title="Color and SMART mapping">

All 12 bodies sit at one mid strength, so the set reads equally light in Light and Dark. The five SMART characters take `color/background/smart/<area>/subtle`, the SMART step made for illustration bodies. The seven math characters take an accent hue at `color/background/accent/<hue>/subtle` and never a SMART hue, so they use red, blue, green and purple, and three hues repeat: red for dot and pi, blue for approximately equal and equal, green for root and divider. <FoundationSection title="Usage">

Use an illustration where the page has room to breathe: empty states, onboarding, and success and completion moments. Decide first that it is needed; it supports the copy and never replaces it. Keep it out of dense working surfaces.

<FoundationSubsection title="When to use">

<RefTable
    head={['Where', 'Illustration', 'Guidance']}
    rows={[
        ['Empty state', 'Yes', 'One character above the message: 96 on a card, 160 on a page.'],
        ['Onboarding and tours', 'Yes', 'One character for each step: 160 or 240.'],
        ['Completion and encouragement', 'Yes', 'A finished session or a met goal: 160 or 240.'],
        ['SMART area header', 'Yes', 'The area’s own character, at 48 or 96.'],
        ['Tables, forms and toolbars', 'No', 'These are working surfaces. Try icons, copy or another affordance before an illustration.'],
        ['Inline errors and buttons', 'No', 'These need words, not mood. Use a Banner.'],
    ]}
/>

</FoundationSubsection>

<FoundationSubsection title="Rules">

- Use a Character instance; never detach it or recolor its parts. Color comes from the tokens, so Light and Dark follow.
- Pick a size from the scale (48, 96, 160 or 240) and scale uniformly.
- Limbs reach past the 1000 frame, so leave room around the instance and do not clip it.
- The math characters do not stand for a SMART area. Their accent hues are free choices; never reuse a SMART character's color on them.
- Reuse the existing characters first. A new character, or a new kind of illustration, is a design lead's call.
- An illustration works with the copy and never replaces it. Art with no job to do stays out of working surfaces; too much of it adds to what a reader has to take in.
- Other visual aids have their own homes: Avatar for people, Icon for actions with labels, Logo for brand, Image for photos.

</FoundationSubsection>

<FoundationSection title="Accessibility">

- Decorative by default. In code, a character with no `title` is hidden from screen readers.
- When a character carries meaning the text around it does not, give it a `title` that names the idea, not the drawing: "Technology and tools", not "brown equals sign with arms".
- Never put the only copy of information in an illustration.
- Line work uses `color/icon/subtle`, about 2.6:1 on the bodies in Light, as in the source art. Decorative art is not held to 3:1, so keep illustrations decorative.

</FoundationSection>
