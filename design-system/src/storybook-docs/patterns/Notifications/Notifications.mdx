## The carriers

Four components carry every message. Each component page holds its appearances; this pattern picks the carrier and the place. A Modal is the fifth, for the one message that must be answered first.

## Banner tiers

One banner at a time, scrolling with its content. The Frame tier is system news and pins only during an active outage. The Page tier is about this page. The Section tier is about this collection. The Frame and Page `banner` slots are Planned <PlannedTag />: today a Banner goes in the content, set where its tier says.

## Usage

Use Notifications when the product needs to tell someone something: an action finished, a condition holds, a decision is needed. Match the disruption to the urgency: a Flag passes, a SectionMessage stays in the page, a Banner holds for a condition, a Modal interrupts. Keep notifications to what is needed; when every message shouts, none does.

<DocsTable>
<thead>
<tr><th>The message is about…</th><th>Carrier</th><th>Where</th><th>Leaves</th></tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td>Something that already happened and needs no reply: saved, sent, copied</td><td>`Flag`</td><td>Top right</td><td>On its own after 8 s; warning and danger on Dismiss</td></tr>
<tr><td>A job that finished or failed after the person moved on</td><td>`Flag` with an action</td><td>Top right</td><td>On Dismiss</td></tr>
<tr><td>Something to read or act on in this part of the page</td><td>`SectionMessage`</td><td>Top of the Section or form</td><td>Resolved or dismissed</td></tr>
<tr><td>Guidance before a task that must not be missed</td><td>`SectionMessage`, dismiss off</td><td>By the action it informs</td><td>When it stops applying</td></tr>
<tr><td>A condition that holds for everything in a Frame, page or Section</td><td>`Banner` at that tier</td><td>Top of the tier</td><td>When the condition ends</td></tr>
<tr><td>A note beside one thing that most people never need</td><td>`InlineMessage`</td><td>Beside the field or value</td><td>On close</td></tr>
<tr><td>Something that must be answered before going on</td><td>`Modal`, Acknowledge or Active</td><td>Centered over a Backdrop</td><td>On the button</td></tr>
<tr><td>What arrived over time</td><td>The Inbox, a Panel of the Notifications type</td><td>A floating Panel, no Backdrop</td><td>When read</td></tr>
</tbody>

## Best practices

### Place Flags at the top right, two lines at most.

<RuleTag kind="automatic" />

A title that names the event and one line for the next step. A `FlagGroup` stacks them newest on top and renders with the page so each one is announced. `FlagGroup` takes `position="top-end"` today; the top right as its default is Planned <PlannedTag />.

### Put nothing important only in a Flag.

<RuleTag kind="review" />

A Flag can be dismissed before it is read. A failure that needs a decision stays on the page in a SectionMessage; a decision that blocks the task is a Modal.

<DoDontPair
    doExample={<FlagDo />}
    doCaption="A finished action as a success Flag: the event in the title, the next step below."
    dontExample={<FlagDont />}
    dontCaption="A decision in a Flag, which is gone before it is answered."
/>

### Let routine Flags leave and keep warning and danger until dismissed.

<RuleTag kind="automatic" />

Normal, information and success close after 8 s. Warning and error stay until dismissed. A Flag with an action keeps autohide only when the action is optional.

### Show one Banner at a time, one line, at the tier it concerns, for as long as the condition holds.

<RuleTag kind="automatic" />

Frame for system news, pinned only during an active outage <PlannedTag />; Page for this page; Section for this collection. A Banner has no dismiss and no timeout, so it is mounted only while the condition is true, and it scrolls with the content.

<DoDontPair
    doExample={<BannerDo />}
    doCaption="One Banner, one line, at the tier it concerns."
    dontExample={<BannerDont />}
    dontCaption="Two Banners stacked, pushing the page down."
/>

### Keep a SectionMessage in the page until it is resolved or dismissed.

<RuleTag kind="automatic" />

The fill and icon carry the appearance, with no border and no side stripe. A blocking message turns dismiss off. Put up to two actions after the body. Field errors stay on the field.

### Match the appearance to the meaning and say it in the words.

<RuleTag kind="review" />

Success for a finished action, warning for a caution, danger only when something failed. The icon is hidden from screen readers, so the title says "saved", "couldn't", "due".

### Keep the bold Flag for the one message that must be seen.

<RuleTag kind="review" />

A bold fill competes with the page. Routine news stays quiet.

### Send what arrived over time to the Inbox.

<RuleTag kind="review" />

A Flag is for now; the Inbox is for later. The Inbox is the Panel's Notifications type: items by time, each unread one marked with a dot and the word. The Bell in the Sidebar carrying the count, and the Panel growing out of the Sidebar from the left, are Planned <PlannedTag />.

## Content guidelines

<DocsTable>
<thead>
<tr><th>Do</th><th>Don't</th></tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td>Flag title names the event: "Session logged."</td><td>"Success!"</td></tr>
<tr><td>Flag line gives the next step: "Maya's supervisor will see it Friday."</td><td>A bare title with no next step.</td></tr>
<tr><td>Banner is one sentence: "Scheduled maintenance Saturday 2–4 AM ET."</td><td>A paragraph in the Banner.</td></tr>
<tr><td>Own the problem: "We lost the connection. Your work is kept on this device."</td><td>"Connection lost by client."</td></tr>
<tr><td>Actions are verbs: "Undo", "View sessions".</td><td>"Click here", "OK".</td></tr>
</tbody>

## Accessibility

- Warning and error Flags are alerts and interrupt; every other Flag is a polite status. Each is read as one message.
- A `SectionMessage` announces assertively when it appears; one present at first paint is ordinary content.
- A `Banner` is a status that never traps focus and sits in the reading order.
- The timeout on a Flag is a timing limit; its Dismiss button satisfies it.
- The appearance icon is decorative; the words carry the severity.
