## For developers

- **The value is ISO.** <code>value</code> and <code>onChange</code> speak YYYY-MM-DD, and the closed field displays MM/DD/YYYY. The two are not the same string, so do not read what is on screen as the value. A slashed date passed as <code>value</code> parses to empty and the field shows its placeholder.
- **A range is one picker.** <code>value</code> and <code>onChange</code> speak a start and an end, and either may be empty while picking. The first click sets the first day and the second sets the last. A click before the first day starts again. Typed, a range is two dates joined by a dash.
- **Typed dates are checked when the person is done.** The field is checked on Enter or when focus leaves it. A real day inside the span is picked and <code>onChange</code> fires. Anything else stays as typed, the field is drawn invalid, and <code>onChange</code> is not called, so the last good value is still yours.
- **The calendar opens 4 below the field**, with its left edge on the field's and at least as wide. With no room below it opens above, the same gap away. <code>calendarAlign</code> moves it to the center or the right, and near the right edge of the viewport it right-aligns on its own. Every field popup follows this rule, Select included.
- **Bounds disable days and do not clamp a value.** A <code>value</code> outside <code>minDate</code> and <code>maxDate</code> that you pass in is still displayed. Validate the incoming value yourself.
- **It gives you a date and not a time.** <code>onChange</code> hands back the ISO day and nothing else. For a moment in time use Date time picker.

## When to use

Use a Date picker when the answer is a calendar day and the weekday matters, such as when a session happens or which day a report covers. Turn on <code>range</code> when the answer has a first and a last day. For a day and a time together, use [Date time picker](?path=/docs/components-forms-and-inputs-date-time-picker--docs). For a choice among a few fixed dates, a [Select](?path=/docs/components-forms-and-inputs-select--docs) is shorter.

- **Pick a span with one range picker.** A range is one field with two dates, so the calendar can show the span and stop an end before the start. Two separate pickers cannot.
- **Set the earliest and latest dates.** Disable the days nobody may pick, such as past sessions, with <code>minDate</code> and <code>maxDate</code>. Check typed dates against the same span when the field loses focus, and say the allowed span in the message.
- **Keep the format as the placeholder.** MM/DD/YYYY tells people what to type. Do not replace it with an instruction such as "Pick a date", because the label says what the date is for.
- **Keep the field typeable.** For a known, distant date such as a birth date, typing beats paging through months.

## Accessibility

- **The field says what it opens.** It has the role combobox with a dialog popup, an expanded state, and a link to the calendar. Down opens the calendar from the keyboard, Escape closes it, and Enter checks what was typed.
- **The days are real buttons.** A day outside the allowed span is genuinely disabled, so it is reachable by Tab and announced as unavailable and not left doing nothing.
- **A day announces its whole date.** Each button is named with the weekday, day, month and year in the reader's locale. It is marked pressed when it is the chosen day, and current when it is today.
- **Adjacent-month days are dimmed and still readable.** They measure 7.66:1 against the calendar surface, against 13.98:1 for the current month, and both pass the 4.5:1 that 14px text needs.
- **There is no arrow-key movement inside the calendar.** Tab steps through the days one at a time, which is up to 31 stops to the end of a month. Typing the date is the faster path, and the field accepts it.
- **A rejected date is announced.** The input is marked invalid when what was typed is not a day it can take.
- **In a range, the ends and the span are marked.** Both ends are marked pressed, and the days between are drawn as in range.
- **Read-only and disabled both stop the calendar opening.** Disabled takes the input out of the tab order. Read-only leaves it focusable and inert, which is the difference between "not for you" and "not now".
