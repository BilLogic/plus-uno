<FoundationSection title="Drawing rules">

- Paint with design-system tokens only: color, stroke and corner. The kit follows light and dark on its own.
- Pick each example's fidelity by what the lesson depends on. Remove every word in your head: if the lesson still holds, draw it abstract.
  - **Concrete:** the lesson is a component's look, states, behavior or wording, such as anatomy, states, content do's and don'ts, or an interaction. Use a live story with real content, not a drawing.
  - **Real subject:** the lesson is one element inside a screen, such as which action is primary, a label, or one piece's placement. Draw that element real, with its words, and keep everything around it blocked out.
  - **Blocked out:** the lesson is structure across screens, such as layout options, which surface opens, alignment, phases, or many variants side by side. Use no words, and draw buttons as flat strips.
  - Motion follows the same split. A component's own motion is a live story. A principle such as easing, choreography or stagger animates blocked-out shapes. One element moving inside a screen animates the real subject over a blocked-out context.
- Draw skeleton bars where text would sit. Words appear only on a real subject; the rest goes in the caption beside the drawing.
- Draw buttons only in shipped appearances: primary, the brand fill, on the subject; default, the neutral fill, everywhere else.
- Keep one scale per set and equal margins around every drawing, balanced by eye.
- Give each drawing one accent, and place it on the subject.

</FoundationSection>
