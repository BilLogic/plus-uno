It is never decoration: each cue answers what floats, what groups and what you can press.

<Callout title="Why two cues">

Surface color and shadow do one job: they build the sense of layers, so you can tell what sits above what and what you can act on. Each cue covers what the others cannot.

- **Surface color** separates before any shadow: Sunken groups, Raised and overlay surfaces read as a different plane.
- **Shadow** says how high: contact, then height, stronger for each layer up.

Used together, they make one layer read the same way everywhere.

</Callout>

<FoundationSection title="Golden rules">

1. Flat by default.
2. Lift means you can act on it.
3. Raise one thing per view.
4. Group with a surface color or a border, never a shadow.
5. A blocking overlay sits on the Blanket.
6. One blocking overlay at a time.
7. No stroke on an elevated surface.
8. Pair the surface with its shadow.
9. Dark mode lightens only what floats.
10. A control’s boundary meets 3:1.

Each rule is drawn as a Do and a Don’t under Usage.

</FoundationSection>
