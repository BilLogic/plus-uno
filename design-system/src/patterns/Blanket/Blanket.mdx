## Usage

A Blanket interrupts the page for one task. It dims everything behind it with one fill, so the person sees only the layer on top. It never draws content of its own.

<DocsTable>
<thead>
<tr><th>Layer</th><th>When to use</th></tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td>Modal</td><td>One task blocks the page. The Blanket centers it.</td></tr>
<tr><td>Drawer</td><td>The task needs the page in view. The Blanket holds it at an edge.</td></tr>
<tr><td>Overlay Panel</td><td>The Frame is too narrow to dock the Panel. A <code>contained</code> Blanket covers the Frame's Page and Sidebar, with the same fill and layer order as a modal's.</td></tr>
</tbody>
</DocsTable>

The Blanket draws the layer only. It does not trap focus, close on Escape or count toward the two-overlay limit. For a real dialog, use the core Modal component, which draws the same layers and blanket token. The Drawer pattern brings its own focus handling. `inline` draws the Blanket in the page flow at a stand-in screen of `screenWidth` and `screenHeight`, for docs and previews.

## Parts

1. **Blanket.** The screen-size layer with the blanket fill, the screen grid and the pads.
2. **Layer.** The one modal or drawer it holds, centered or at an edge. The Modal brings its body and footer slots.

The Blanket carries the screen grid; page frames carry the content grid. Height, hug or fill, lives on the Modal inside it.

### States

A Blanket has no states. It is shown while its layer is open and gone when it closes; a click on the blanket, not on the layer, calls `onBlanketClick`.

## Best practices

One blanket, one overlay at a time.

### One blanket per screen.

A dialog never opens a second Blanket. When a follow-up decision comes up inside a modal, such as confirming a delete, the modal swaps its title, body and footer for an inline confirmation in the same Blanket.

<DoDontPair
    doExample={<OneBlanketDo />}
    doCaption="One blanket per screen."
    dontExample={<OneBlanketDont />}
    dontCaption="Stack a second blanket."
/>

### Confirm in a small modal.

<DoDontPair
    doExample={<ConfirmSmallDo />}
    doCaption="Confirm in a small modal."
    dontExample={<ConfirmSmallDont />}
    dontCaption="Confirm in a large modal."
/>

### One overlay at a time.

Opening a modal closes a floating sidebar, so the two never stack. A floating sidebar gets a shadow and no blanket, and popups, flags and tooltips never bring one.

<DoDontPair
    doExample={<OneOverlayDo />}
    doCaption="One overlay at a time."
    dontExample={<OneOverlayDont />}
    dontCaption="Open a drawer and a modal together."
/>

### Place a modal in the Blanket.

Let the Blanket place the modal. Do not detach it to move things around: the pads are the closest a modal gets to the screen edge.

## Content guidelines

The Blanket holds no text of its own. The words belong to the layer it holds.

## Accessibility

- The Blanket is a layout layer with no role. The Modal or Drawer inside it is the dialog.
- The blanket is a pointer shortcut, not a control. A keyboard closes a dialog from inside it, so the blanket takes no role and no key handler.
- The blanket at `--ds-blanket` keeps the dialog legible in Light and Dark. The toolbar's Contrast switch changes it with the rest of the page.
- A layer wider than a narrow screen keeps its width, and the Blanket scrolls.
