crests, lesson thumbnails and the icon beside a list item. Anything that would
need an icon larger than 24 belongs in a Tile. Its corners are a quarter of its
size.

## Anatomy

1. **Ground.** The square, filled from the color. Subtle is the color's container with its on-container icon. Bold is the solid color with its on-color icon. The corners are a quarter of the size.
2. **Content.** An icon, a logo or an image. Icons and logos are inset. An image fills the square and crops.
3. **Border.** An optional 1px outline-variant edge inside the tile, for a logo or an image whose own edge fades into the page.

## Rules

- **Pick the content, then the color.** Icons take `appearance` and `color`. Logos take a ground color only, since the logo keeps its own. Images cover the tile, so color does not apply.
- **Colors are categories.** Keep one color per kind of item across the app, or leave tiles gray. A color never stands for a status.
- **Frame a tool logo or a list-item icon in a Tile.** A loose square leaves size, corner and color to chance. The Tile sets all three.
- **Never round a tile by hand.** The corners are a quarter of the size.
- **Icons are Font Awesome Free names.** Use `fa-solid`, `fa-regular` or `fa-brands`.

</div>

## For developers

Import `Tile` from the `@/components` barrel. Pick the content first, then the
props that content takes.

```jsx
// Incorrect: a bold appearance on a logo does nothing, and a color on an
// image is hidden under the picture.
<Tile content="logo" appearance="bold" src={logoUrl} />
<Tile content="image" color="blue" src={photoUrl} />

// Correct.
<Tile icon="book-open" appearance="bold" color="blue" />
<Tile content="logo" src={logoUrl} />
<Tile content="image" src={photoUrl} />
```

`color` takes the Tag color set. `orange` and `grey` are deprecated aliases for
`yellow` and `gray`. They render the same and log a warning in development.

## When to use

Use a Tile wherever a small square picture stands for a thing: a tool's logo in
a list of integrations, a school's crest beside its name, a lesson's thumbnail,
or an icon marking the kind of item in a row. Putting all of them in one frame
keeps them the same size and shape wherever they appear.

- For a glyph beside text at 12, 16 or 24, use an **Icon** on its own.
- For a person, use **Avatar**. People are round and named, and a tile is square.
- For a value someone picked, with a label, use **Tag**.

</div>

<div className="sb-ds-doc-section">

## Accessibility

- **Decorative by default.** A tile usually sits beside a name that already says what it is, so it is hidden from assistive technology unless it has a `label`.
- **A label makes it one image with a name.** The tile becomes a `role="img"` with that name, and the picture inside stays hidden, so the square is announced once. Give a tile a label whenever it stands alone, such as a logo with no name beside it.
- **Not a control.** A tile has no hover or focus state. To make one clickable, wrap it in a button or link that carries the accessible name and the focus ring.
- **Color is never the only signal.** The name beside the tile says what the category is.

</div>
