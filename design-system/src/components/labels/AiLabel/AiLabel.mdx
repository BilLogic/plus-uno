## Usage

Use `AiLabel` wherever a person is looking at something the agent had a hand in:
a suggested question, a drafted summary, a recommended lesson. It answers two
questions at once (where did this come from, and how much should I trust it),
and both belong on the content rather than in a paragraph elsewhere on the page.

### Origin guidance

<DocsTable>
<thead>
<tr><th>Origin</th><th>When to use</th></tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td>Generated</td><td>The agent's own output. Default words: "Generated by AI".</td></tr>
<tr><td>Assisted</td><td>A person's work the agent helped with. Default words: "Written with AI".</td></tr>
<tr><td>Reviewed</td><td>Agent output a person has since checked. Default words: "AI draft, reviewed".</td></tr>

It is a **first stab, not a settled component.** The color is
deliberately unresolved: `--plus-ai-accent` is one local custom property behind a
neutral token, so when the brand decision is made it lands on a single
declaration.

## Parts

1. **Mark.** The wand-sparkles glyph, decoration. Its accent follows the origin: neutral for generated and assisted, the success color for reviewed, the warning color at low confidence.
2. **Label.** Says who authored the value: "Generated by AI", "Written with AI", "AI draft, reviewed".
3. **Note.** At low confidence only: what to do about it. Defaults to "Check this before relying on it".
4. **Explain button.** Optional: a real button inside the mark, named for what it does.

## Best practices

### Say who acted.

Origin names who authored the value; reviewed means a person checked it.

<DoDontPair
    doExample={<div style={{ display: 'flex', flexWrap: 'wrap', alignItems: 'center', gap: 'var(--inline-md)' }}><span>Draft, not checked</span><AiLabel origin="generated" /></div>}
    doCaption="An unchecked draft says the AI wrote it."
    dontExample={<div style={{ display: 'flex', flexWrap: 'wrap', alignItems: 'center', gap: 'var(--inline-md)' }}><span>Draft, not checked</span><AiLabel origin="reviewed" /></div>}
    dontCaption="Reviewed on a draft no person has checked."
/>

### Mark authorship, not technology.

`origin` separates the agent's own output from a person's work it helped with,
and both from output a person has since checked. A reader needs those three
apart far more than they need to know a model was involved somewhere.

<DoDontPair
    doExample={<AiLabel origin="generated" />}
    doCaption="Say who authored it, and the default words follow."
    dontExample={<AiLabel text="AI-powered" />}
    dontCaption={'"AI was here" is not information a reader can act on.'}
/>

### Show low confidence.

Low confidence adds the caution treatment so people double-check.

<DoDontPair
    doExample={<div style={{ display: 'flex', flexWrap: 'wrap', alignItems: 'center', gap: 'var(--inline-md)' }}><span>Answer may be wrong</span><AiLabel origin="generated" confidence="low" /></div>}
    doCaption="Low confidence on a shaky answer."
    dontExample={<div style={{ display: 'flex', flexWrap: 'wrap', alignItems: 'center', gap: 'var(--inline-md)' }}><span>Answer may be wrong</span><AiLabel origin="generated" /></div>}
    dontCaption="High confidence on a shaky answer."
/>

### Say what to do at low confidence.

`confidence="low"` changes the treatment and the accessible name. It also
carries a note, and the default note is an instruction rather than a label:
a caution with no instruction is the thing this is meant to prevent.

<DoDontPair
    doExample={<AiLabel origin="generated" confidence="low" note="Two citations could not be verified" />}
    doCaption="The note says what is wrong and what to do."
    dontExample={<AiLabel origin="generated" confidence="low" note="Low confidence" />}
    dontCaption="The reader is told to worry and not told what about."
/>

### Use one label per output.

The label marks the whole output, not each sentence inside it.

<DoDontPair
    doExample={<div style={{ display: 'flex', flexWrap: 'wrap', alignItems: 'center', gap: 'var(--inline-md)' }}><span>Summary</span><AiLabel origin="generated" /></div>}
    doCaption="One label for the output."
    dontExample={<div style={{ display: 'flex', flexDirection: 'column', gap: 'var(--stack-md)' }}><div style={{ display: 'flex', flexWrap: 'wrap', alignItems: 'center', gap: 'var(--inline-md)' }}><span>Sentence one</span><AiLabel origin="generated" /></div><div style={{ display: 'flex', flexWrap: 'wrap', alignItems: 'center', gap: 'var(--inline-md)' }}><span>Sentence two</span><AiLabel origin="generated" /></div><div style={{ display: 'flex', flexWrap: 'wrap', alignItems: 'center', gap: 'var(--inline-md)' }}><span>Sentence three</span><AiLabel origin="generated" /></div></div>}
    dontCaption="A label on every sentence."
/>

### Keep the mark out of controls.

`onExplain` renders one real button inside it. Nesting one interactive element
inside another is invalid per ARIA. To make a whole row pressable, put a `Button` around the content and
this inside it.

</div>

## Accessibility

- **The name says what the mark means, not what it is.** The surface this
  replaces is labeled `AI-powered feature`, which tells a screen-reader user
  the category of the thing and nothing about the content it is attached to.
- **The confidence is in the accessible name.** Somebody who cannot see the
  caution treatment is otherwise told the same thing whether or not the system
  trusts its own output.
- **`role="note"`** makes the mark one thing rather than a loose icon beside
  loose words, so it is announced as a phrase.
- **The glyph is decorative and hidden.** It repeats what the words already say;
  announcing it would give "wand, Generated by AI" and no extra
  information, the same rule `Tag`'s `swatchBefore` follows.
- **The explain button says what it does.** `How was this made?` is an action,
  not a category. Compare `title="AI recommended"`, which is not a reliable
  accessible name in any screen reader.
- **Low confidence uses the tinted warning background with warning text, never
  the bold ground.** A bold fill would flood the surface the mark sits on, and
  the tinted pair is the one every other warning label reads, so it holds in
  light, dark and at more contrast.

## Related

- For a value a person chose rather than one the agent produced, use `Tag`.
- For system-generated data with no trust question attached, use `StatusTag` or `CountTag`.
- For a message about the whole page rather than a mark on one thing, use `SectionMessage`.
- For work still in progress, use `Spinner` or `ProgressBar`.
