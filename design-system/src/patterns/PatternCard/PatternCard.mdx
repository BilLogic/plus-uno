The card pattern is a raised box that holds one object’s content in slots: an optional image, a header, a body and a footer. The pattern owns the frame and the Card spacing tokens, and you fill the slots. It follows the Pattern/Card page in Figma.

Use the card pattern for one block you compose yourself, such as a table, a chart or a form with its own header. Use the <a href="?path=/docs/components-layout-and-structure-card--docs">Card component</a> for one item among peers, such as a session, a tutor or a program, when the content fits its props: an image, a title, text, a list, links and one action.

## When to use

Use the card pattern to frame content you compose yourself on a page: a header with actions, then a table, a chart, a form or a short list. Use it when the card needs the footer, the sizes or the lift of a clickable card.

For a self-contained item that you fill by props (an image, a title, text, links and one action), use the Card component. For a whole region of a page rather than one object, use Section. For a page’s one main message, use Jumbotron. For records that share columns, use a table.

Colors follow the toolbar’s Color mode and Contrast switches. In dark, the raised surface is a lighter gray than the page.

## Rules

- **Pick the size by what the card holds,** then let the row decide the count. Place the most of 4, 3, 2 or 1 cards per row that keeps each card at its minimum width: 224, 304 or 480.
- **Let pad and radius follow the size.** Padding is 16, 20 or 24. Radius is 12 for small and 16 for medium and large.
- **Choose stack and inline by use,** whatever the size: 4, 8 and 12.
- **Give a card with commit actions a footer.** Card actions start on the left, under the content. Only a modal’s footer aligns right.
- **Run the image edge to edge** across the top, outside the padding, clipped by the card radius.
- **Leave the header off only when the body carries its own title,** such as a metric tile.
- **Keep cards raised at rest, with no outline.**

## Accessibility

- **The title is a heading,** tagged one level below the section the card sits in. Keep a card out of the heading outline by leaving the title unset.
- **A clickable card needs its semantics added.** Give it a role, a tab stop and a key handler, or put a real button inside it. The card itself adds none.
- **Do not rest meaning on the raised fill.** The surface and its shadow separate the card from the page. Text and icons keep their contrast in every Color mode and Contrast setting.
- **Name an icon-only action.** Buttons in the actions slot carry a label that names the card, such as “More actions for Algebra I”.
