## Anatomy

A Section with its header rows, a detail and a footer. The title, the first pill and the first column share one left edge.

<ComponentExample of={SectionStories.AnatomyQueue} />

1. **Title row.** The h2, an optional one-line description, and the actions at the end: More and the labeled Primary.
2. **Header rows** (optional). Row 2 holds the pill views at the start and the format switch at the end. Row 3 holds Search, then the filter triggers.
3. **Body.** A Table, a card grid, a list, a form or subsections. A Table's head row follows the header rows with nothing between.
4. **Detail** (optional). An inline expand, drawn as a sunken Card, that opens in place inside the Section and pushes the content below it down. Never use it for a queue page, which docks a `Panel` beside the list.
5. **Footer** (optional). Paging or "View all".

## Header rows

A starting set, picked by what the Section holds. Every control state lives on its component: <DocsLink id="components-forms-and-inputs-button-icon-button">Icon button</DocsLink>, <DocsLink id="components-forms-and-inputs-button-filter-button">Filter button</DocsLink> and <DocsLink id="components-navigation-tabs">Tabs</DocsLink>. Which filters the header takes is <DocsLink id="patterns-filtering">Filtering</DocsLink>; how the search field behaves is the Search pattern, listed in <DocsLink id="patterns-overview">Patterns</DocsLink>.

A header has up to three rows, all on one 32 line. Row 1 is the title with its actions: usually one Primary standing alone, with a `…` Icon button or a secondary button beside it. Row 2 is the views at the start and the format switch at the end. Row 3 is Search, then the filter triggers, each reading "Field: value". A Section with no views drops row 2 and the format switch moves to the end of row 3; one with nothing to search or filter drops row 3.

Every control in a header row is 32: Buttons, Icon buttons and Filter buttons at default spacing, Search and text fields at compact spacing. A Tag beside them is 20. Applied filters take the selected state, and the row ends with the count and Clear all. Three triggers show and the rest go in More filters with a count. Under 768 Search takes the row and the triggers fold into one Filter button with a count; the count and Clear all stay at the end of the Search row. When the triggers no longer fit beside Search they wrap to their own row, end-aligned. A trigger can open a list: give its `filters` entry a `menu` that hands the trigger to a Popup select or a Filter menu.

## Usage

Use `Section` when content has a heading, shares actions, shows or hides as a unit, or takes its own span of the grid; otherwise the content belongs to the Section above it. The header is the Section's, not the Table's: the title row names it and carries its actions, and the header rows under it hold the views, then Search and the filters, so switching views can swap a table for cards while the rows stay put.

A Section takes no fill and no pad, and has five parts: header, body, side, detail and footer. The detail is an inline expand, drawn as a sunken Card, that opens in place inside the Section and pushes the content below it down.

</div>

<div className="sb-ds-doc-section">

## Alternatives

- For one object with an agreed shape, use `Card` inside a Section.
- For a queue page or a quick look at a row from a table, use `Panel`.
- For the page's own title, use `PageHeader`; a Section stands under it.

</div>

## Best practices

### Make a Section only when it earns one.

<RuleTag kind="review" />

A Section earns its own heading, its own actions or its own place in the grid. When its heading would repeat its only card's title, drop the Section and let the card stand on its own.

<DoDontPair
    doExample={<CardStandsAlone />}
    doCode={CODE.CardStandsAlone}
    doCaption="A card with its own title stands on its own in the page."
    dontExample={<SectionRepeatsCard />}
    dontCode={CODE.SectionRepeatsCard}
    dontCaption="A Section whose heading repeats its only card's title: two headings for one thing."
/>

### Put the title row first and the header rows under it.

<RuleTag kind="automatic" />

The title row sits `--section-stack-sm` above the header rows, and the header sits `--section-stack-md` above the body. The title, the first pill's label and the first column's text share one left edge. Actions that do not fit beside the heading drop under it, still at the end of the row. At every width the two stay separate rows. In a narrow Section the filters fold into one Filter button and the views scroll sideways.

<DoDontPair
    doExample={<TitleRowAboveHeaderRows />}
    doCode={CODE.TitleRowAboveHeaderRows}
    doCaption="The title row first, the header rows under it, sharing one left edge."
    dontExample={<TitleInViewsRow />}
    dontCode={CODE.TitleInViewsRow}
    dontCaption="The title pushed into the views row: the heading and the pills fight for the start, and the description has nowhere to go."
/>

### Give each action one home: the title row.

<RuleTag kind="review" />

Actions sit at the end of the title row, More then the labeled Primary, whether or not the Section has views. The views row is for views and the format switch, the third for Search and filters, so an action there is a second home.

<DoDontPair
    doExample={<ActionsInTitleRow />}
    doCode={CODE.ActionsInTitleRow}
    doCaption="The actions at the end of the title row, with the views under it."
    dontExample={<ActionsInTwoHomes />}
    dontCode={CODE.ActionsInTwoHomes}
    dontCaption="Add students in the title row and again in the views row: one action, two homes."
/>

### Keep views at the start as pills that hug their labels.

<RuleTag kind="review" />

Views are a fixed set the product defines, and one pill stays even with one view. The strip stays on one line and scrolls sideways when the pills do not fit. Under page tabs, the views become one labeled PopupSelect, so the page keeps one tab system.

### Order the header: actions, then views and format, then Search and filters.

<RuleTag kind="review" />

The title row ends with More, then the labeled Primary, which is last. The views row holds the views at the start and the format switch at the end. The filters row holds a compact `SearchField` (`spacing="compact"`, and `isCollapsible` where the row is tight), then the filter triggers; a trigger that opens a list takes a `menu`. More is an Icon button with a Tooltip. Fold extras into More.

<DoDontPair
    doExample={<RightSideInOrder />}
    doCode={CODE.RightSideInOrder}
    doCaption="More and the labeled Primary in the title row; the format switch ends the views row; Search, then the filter, lead the filters row."
    dontExample={<RightSideCrowded />}
    dontCode={CODE.RightSideCrowded}
    dontCaption="Five controls in no order, the Primary first, a second labeled button and a Filter icon in the title row: fold extras into More and give the filter its own row."
/>

### Turn the head row into the bulk bar when rows are selected.

<RuleTag kind="automatic" />

The part-ticked select-all, the count of selected rows, then the actions, all at the start. The checkboxes clear the selection, so the bar carries no Cancel. Selected rows take the selected fill and the header rows stay put. Only the Dynamic table selects rows.

### Drop the Filter icon while a filter side shows.

<RuleTag kind="review" />

Each control has one home. The Filter button returns with its count once the side hides, below `SECTION_DETAIL_FLOOR`.

### Use a Section detail as an inline expand, never on a queue page.

<RuleTag kind="review" />

A **Section detail** is an inline expand: a row or card opens in place inside its Section and pushes the content below it down. It is never the detail view on a queue page — a queue page docks a `Panel` beside the list instead, and the record opens on demand.

### Keep the footer for paging and "View all".

<RuleTag kind="review" />

Actions that commit changes belong in the card or form that holds the fields.

<DoDontPair
    doExample={<CardOwnsSave />}
    doCode={CODE.CardOwnsSave}
    doCaption={'The form card holds its own Save; the Section footer is for paging and "View all".'}
    dontExample={<FooterCommits />}
    dontCode={CODE.FooterCommits}
    dontCaption="Cancel and Save in the Section footer, away from the fields they commit."
/>

### Give a part a fill with a Card, not a Section.

<RuleTag kind="review" />

A padded box with a fill is a Card. A Section takes no fill and no pad, so a part that stands apart from its neighbor, such as a detail, is a sunken Card.

### Nest a Section once at most.

<RuleTag kind="review" />

A subsection is an h3. A Section that already has views holds subsections without them.

The table's empty state replaces the whole table, by the Empty pattern; elevation and fills follow <DocsLink id="foundations-visual-elevation-overview">Elevation</DocsLink>.

## Accessibility

- The title is a heading whose level follows nesting: h2 for a Section, h3 for a subsection. With a custom header the title stays in the page, hidden from sight.
- The pill views are a tablist; the selected tab says which rows the body shows. Views swap in place.
- Search, the filter triggers and More carry their names: "Search students", "Status: Upcoming", "More section actions". A trigger's selected state is announced as pressed.
- The table announces a selection politely, "2 selected", and the bulk bar's actions follow the select-all checkbox in the tab order.
- The detail is a collapsible region named by its title: the disclosure button toggles it with Enter or Space, and Escape inside the open detail closes it and returns focus to the button.
