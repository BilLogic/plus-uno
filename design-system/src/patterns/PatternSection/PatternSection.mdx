A section is a span of the page grid that groups content under one heading. It has no fill, no pad and no size options: the Surface around it supplies the pad, so a section never draws a box of its own. The Section header comes first, then the body, then an optional footer.

The examples on this page follow the toolbar's Color mode switch.

### When to use

Make a section when the content has a **heading**, shares **actions**, shows or hides as a unit, or takes its own span of the grid. Otherwise the content is part of the section above it.

Do not wrap a single card in a section that repeats the card's own title. A section holds cards, tables, rows, forms or subsections, and never a card inside a card.

A subsection is a section inside a section. Use two levels at most. Use `layout="subsections"` when one page holds several related groups that each need a title, such as a settings page with Profile, Notifications and Privacy.

When something else fits better:

- For one object, such as a student, a session or a metric, use a Card.
- For a region that needs a fill or an edge of its own, use a Surface.
- For rows and columns of data, put a Table inside the section. The section header carries the table's search, filters and actions.

### Anatomy

<DocsCanvasShell attachSourceBelow description={<>A section's parts, top to bottom. A slot is a place you fill with your own content.</>}>
    <DsCanvasQuiet of={PatternSectionStories.Anatomy} />
</DocsCanvasShell>

1. **Section header** is a part of its own with a title, a description and an actions slot, and an optional bottom row for tabs or a toolbar. Its level sets the heading size.
2. **Content** is a slot. It holds cards, tables, rows or forms.
3. **Footer** is an optional slot at the end, for "View all" or paging.
4. **Side** is a second content slot, used only by <code>layout="split"</code>.

Parts stack Section/stack-md 16 apart. Inside the header, the title row sits Section/stack-sm 8 above the tabs or toolbar row.

### Header types

Five types set what the header holds. The type is read from the slots you fill: <code>header</code> makes it custom, <code>toolbar</code> makes it toolbar and <code>tabs</code> makes it tabs. Pass <code>type="tabs-only"</code> to show only the tabs row.

Tabs belong to the section. They are the bottom row of the section header, and each tab's content is the section body. Use tabs for views of the same thing, and keep each label to one or two words. Use the tabs only type when the page title already names the view. The title then hides visually and stays for screen readers.

Actions, tabs and toolbar are slots: drop in buttons, Nav Tabs or a filter row.

### Level

The title is one heading level below what holds it, and one size smaller. A section on a page is an h2 at Heading/medium 20/24. A subsection is an h3 at Heading/small 16/20, and deeper sections keep that size. <code>level="page"</code> makes the title the page header, an h1 at Heading/large 24/28, and the sections inside it become h2.

### Layouts

<code>single</code> is the content alone. <code>split</code> puts the content, span 8 with a minimum of 480, beside <code>side</code>, span 4 with a minimum of 224. When a column would drop below its minimum, they stack. <code>subsections</code> splits one section into named groups that share a single page heading, and stacks them Section/stack-lg 24 apart. Each group is a subsection, an h3 under the section's h2.

### Toolbar

A table gets its controls from the section header above it, never from the table itself. Use the toolbar type for search, filters, the record count and actions, and the tabs type for saved views. When rows are selected, the toolbar switches to the selected state: the selection, bulk actions and Clear, on <code>--ds-background-selected</code>.

### Loading

<code>loading</code> replaces the whole header with skeleton bars and marks the section busy. The bars are a title bar, a description bar and, when the header has one, a bar for the tabs or toolbar row. Tabs only draws just that row bar. The body is the content's to load.

### Accessibility

- The title is a heading, and its level follows nesting: one h1 per page, h2 for a section, h3 for a subsection. Nest sections instead of choosing a tag.
- A section with no title is a wrapper. What it holds keeps the parent's heading level.
- In the custom and tabs-only types the title is hidden from sight but stays in the page, so the section keeps its name. Always give those sections a title.
- A loading section is marked busy, and the skeleton bars are hidden from assistive technology.
- The selected toolbar announces its selection, such as "3 selected", politely.
- Give the tabs row a label that says what the tabs switch, such as "Views".
- Selection is never color alone: the count and the Clear button say it in words.

### For developers

<code>padding</code> and <code>background</code> are deprecated. They still work and warn once in development. <code>padding</code> draws a pad around the section, and <code>background="container"</code> draws a translucent well. The Surface around a section supplies the pad, so leave both off. For a region with a fill of its own, use a Surface or a Card.
