## For developers

- **There is no value readout.** The component renders the label and the slider and nothing else. When the number matters, render it beside the slider and keep it in step with <code>onChange</code>.
- **onChange fires throughout the drag**, not on release, so a controlled slider stays in step with the thumb. Put anything expensive, such as a network call or a chart redraw, behind a debounce or in <code>onBlur</code>.
- **The filled track is drawn from a custom property** the component sets on the input as the value changes. Style the track by reading <code>--value-percent</code>, not by recomputing the position.
- **Extra props land on the input**, so <code>aria-describedby</code>, <code>disabled</code> and <code>data-*</code> reach the control that has the role.

## When to use

Use Range when the position in a known span matters more than the number. Landing on an exact figure by dragging is slow, and on a touch screen it is unreliable.

- **Use it for:** volume, opacity or a rough weighting, where the span has a fixed minimum and maximum.
- **For an exact value**, use [Number input](?path=/docs/components-forms-and-inputs-number-input--docs).
- **For a choice on a short scale with named points**, use [Choice group](?path=/docs/components-forms-and-inputs-choice-group--docs) laid out inline.
- **For stars**, use [Rating](?path=/docs/components-forms-and-inputs-rating--docs).

</div>

<div className="sb-ds-doc-section">

## Accessibility

- **Always give it a label.** A value of 49 says nothing about what is being set. The label is the only thing that names the slider.
- **Hiding the label keeps the name.** <code>showLabel=false</code> clips the label out of sight and keeps it for assistive technology. Use it where the design shows the meaning another way, such as the minus and plus icons on either side of a zoom slider.
- **Say the value in words when the number is not enough.** Pass <code>aria-valuetext</code>, for example "50 percent", when a listener needs more than "50".
- **The span is announced.** <code>min</code> and <code>max</code> become the slider's lowest and highest values, so a screen reader reports the span with no extra attribute.
- **Focus is visible on the thumb.** The 32-high row gives a pointer a generous target.
