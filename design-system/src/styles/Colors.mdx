<FoundationSection title="Overview">

Color in PLUS is a set of tokens, each named for the job it does: the text on a page, the edge of a field, the fill of the primary button. A token has one value in each color mode, so a component that uses the right token is right in light, dark and increased contrast without any work of its own.

```css
color: var(--ds-text-danger);
background: var(--ds-background-danger-subtler);
```

Figma holds them in `color / foundations`, with modes Light, Dark, Light IC and Dark IC; `color / primitives` stay hidden. Code reads them as `--ds-*` custom properties.

</FoundationSection>

<FoundationSection title="About color">

Color distinguishes PLUS and keeps the tutor and student apps consistent. A color is applied with a design token, so rather than choosing a shade you choose the token for the job: color, then the property (text, icon, border, background), then a role, an emphasis level and an interaction state.

Saturated colors carry meaning, highlight UI or tie related UI together. Neutral colors cover most backgrounds, text and shapes and carry no meaning of their own, though they can imply disabled. Alpha colors have varying transparency so UI adapts to the color or elevation under it.

</FoundationSection>

<FoundationSection title="How the tokens are built">

Two layers. Primitives are the raw ramps and stay hidden; never bind one. Semantic tokens point at a primitive and name a job, and they are the only color tokens you bind.

A name reads from the general to the specific: property, role, emphasis, state. In Figma the parts join with slashes, so `color/background/danger/bold/hovered`. In CSS, drop the leading `color` and join the parts with dashes behind `--ds-`, so `--ds-background-danger-bold-hovered`. That is the fill of a bold danger surface under the pointer.

<FoundationSection title="Modes">

PLUS has two color modes, light and dark, and each has an increased contrast version for people who need stronger text and edges. That makes four modes. A fifth setting, auto, follows the operating system: `prefers-color-scheme` picks light or dark, and `prefers-contrast` turns increased contrast on.

- Dark is not light inverted. Raised and overlay surfaces get lighter as they rise, because a shadow is hard to see on a dark page.
- Increased contrast darkens text and meaningful borders in light and lightens them in dark. Text then reaches 7:1, and borders, icons and the focus ring reach 4.5:1. Tints, surfaces and the Blanket stay as they are.
- Tokens map per theme. Design in dark mode by choosing the same token, and never map your own values.
- In code, the page sets `data-color-mode` (light, dark or auto) and `data-contrast-mode` (no-preference, more or auto). No attribute means light at standard contrast.
- In Figma, set the mode on the frame: Light, Dark, Light IC or Dark IC. Each swatch below shows the value the page resolves for its token right now, so switching the toolbar changes it at once.

<FoundationSection title="Roles">

Seven roles carry meaning. Each ships a subtle ground, a bold ground, and text, border and icon colors that pair with them. Pick the role by meaning, never by how the hue looks.

Danger, warning, success, information and discovery each come in four emphasis levels: the default tint for a Banner, subtler for a status label, subtle for a chip that is not interactive, and bold, the filled version. Discovery marks something new or proposed, such as a Suggestion.

<Canvas of={ColorsStories.StatusRoles} sourceState="none" />

Tokens: `color/background/<role>/subtler` or `bold`, `color/text/<role>`, `color/border/<role>` and `color/icon/<role>`. A neutral border that bounds a control takes `color/border/bold`; `color/border/default` is a divider. A bold warning ground is a light yellow, so its text is the dark `color/text/warning/inverse`.

<FoundationSection title="Choosing a role">

A role describes the intention behind a color. Pick the role for the job, then the emphasis.

<RefTable
    head={['Role', 'Use for']}
    rows={[
        [<C>neutral</C>, 'Default text and secondary UI, such as secondary buttons and navigation.'],
        [<C>brand</C>, 'Primary actions and elements that carry the PLUS brand.'],
        [<C>information</C>, 'Informative UI, such as an information icon, or UI that says something is in progress.'],
        [<C>success</C>, 'A favorable outcome, such as a session booked.'],
        [<C>warning</C>, 'Caution that prevents a mistake or error before it happens.'],
        [<C>danger</C>, 'Danger or a serious error state, such as a failed payment.'],
        [<C>discovery</C>, 'Something new, such as onboarding or a new feature.'],
        [<C>accent</C>, 'Color with no meaning tied to it. You should be able to swap one accent for another and the experience stays the same.'],
        [<C>inverse</C>, 'UI that sits on a bold-emphasis background.'],
        [<C>input</C>, 'Form fields. PLUS form fields already carry these tokens.'],
    ]}
/>

- Emphasis is how much contrast a color has against the default surface, from subtlest to boldest. Bolder colors draw more attention than subtle ones.
- Use the inverse tokens (`color/text/inverse`, `color/icon/inverse`, `color/border/inverse`) on bold backgrounds. On a bold warning background, which is yellow, use the warning inverse tokens, which are made to pass WCAG AA.
- Pick by meaning, never by a matching value. A danger fill, its border and its icon are three tokens even when two of them share a value today.

</FoundationSection>

<FoundationSection title="States">

A state steps along the token's own ladder. Hover and press have their own tokens; focus is a ring, never a fill.

- A state takes the token that matches what the element is. A selected element takes `color/background/selected`, and `color/background/selected/hovered` when hovered.
- A row, menu item or sidebar item takes `color/background/neutral/subtle/hovered` and `/pressed`.
- A bold fill takes its own role's hovered and pressed steps, such as `color/background/brand/bold/hovered` or `color/background/danger/bold/pressed`.
- Only an avatar or an image, whose fill cannot change, keeps a translucent overlay: `color/interaction/hovered` and `color/interaction/pressed`.
- There are no icon hovered or pressed tokens. Show an icon's state with a subtle neutral background behind it.
- Keep selected and focused distinct. Selected persists after an interaction to show a current choice; focused shows where keyboard focus is. Use the selected tokens (background, text, icon, border) for selection and `color/border/focused` only for the focus ring. Never use link, brand or focused tokens to show selection. When an element is both, keep the selected treatment and add the ring.
- Hovered and pressed are temporary: use the matching token for the element's current state, and keep a selected item recognizable while it is hovered or pressed.

<Canvas of={ColorsStories.FocusIsARing} sourceState="none" />

<FoundationSubsection title="Field hover and focus">

Hover only hints that a field is interactive. Focus is the signal for keyboard input, and it is a ring around the field, never a change of fill. A field at hover takes `color/background/input/hovered` with the `color/border/input` edge; at focus it keeps its fill and gains a ring in `color/border/focused` outside the field.

</FoundationSubsection>

</FoundationSection>

<FoundationSection title="Partner pairs and contrast">

Every foreground token has a ground it is made for, and the pair clears the WCAG bar in all four modes: 4.5:1 for text and 3:1 for icons, borders and the focus ring, rising to 7:1 and 4.5:1 under increased contrast. Use 3:1 for UI that is essential to understanding. Each ratio below is measured from the rendered colors.

- On a bold fill, text and icons take `color/text/inverse` and `color/icon/inverse`. Warning is the exception: `color/text/warning/inverse`, the dark ink.
- On a status tint, text takes the role's text token, or its bolder step on the subtler and subtle grounds.
- On any surface, text takes `color/text/default`, subtle or subtlest. Never carry meaning by color alone: a status has an icon or a word, and a chart has a legend or direct labels.

<FoundationSection title="Chart order, targets and ranges">

Order and tone carry the reading before anyone hovers. Put the biggest first and the darkest first, and keep targets and ranges out of the bars.

- Sort categories from most to least. An ordinal set such as grades or tiers takes one hue ramp instead of separate hues.
- Darker means more: the strongest tone is the biggest value.
- A target is a green tick across the bar. A range is a green outline on the track. Do not shade bands or write values beside the bar: the numbers go in the hover tooltip.
- Text on a chart fill uses the partner token, `text/inverse` on a bold fill. Separate neighboring marks with a gap or an inverse border, never a second color.

<FoundationSection title="Disabled">

Disabled is a color, not an opacity. Fading a control with opacity makes it unreadable on some grounds and gives every control a different gray.

- Use `color/text/disabled`, `color/background/disabled` and `color/border/disabled`. They are the same in every control and keep their contrast pair in all four modes.
- The opacity token is for images only.
- Disabled still needs a reason a person can find: the session has ended, the form is incomplete. Say it in nearby text instead of leaving the control dead.

- The link color has a pressed step but no hovered step. The underline stays on hover.
- Never use brand, selected or focused colors for a link.

<FoundationSection title="Accessibility">

- Every foreground token is made for a ground, and the pair clears the WCAG bar in all four modes: 4.5:1 for text and 3:1 for icons, borders and the focus ring, rising to 7:1 and 4.5:1 under increased contrast.
- Never carry meaning by color alone: a status has an icon or a word beside its color.
- Warning is the one intent whose edge and glyph differ from its fill: an icon takes `color/icon/warning` and an edge `color/border/warning`, because the yellow fill is 2.87:1 on the darkest surface.
- Keyboard focus shows as a ring, never a fill, and the ring stays 3:1 against the surface.
- The WCAG AA bar: 3:1 for UI essential to understanding the experience and for text 24px or larger (1.4.11), 4.5:1 for text smaller than 24px (1.4.3).
- Pair a selected color with another cue, such as a check mark, underline, border or change in shape, so selection never relies on color alone.
- Check a new screen in all four modes before review: the toolbar's Color mode and Contrast switches set them here.

</FoundationSection>
