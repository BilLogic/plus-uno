## Anatomy

An Active Modal at 8-col with a short form. Every part once; the pad is 16 on every side, the radius 16, and the parts sit 16 apart.

2. **Header.** The title at 20/24, whatever the level around it, and the Close icon button. With tabs, the tab group sits 8 under the title.
3. **Body.** Text, a list, fields or a table. It scrolls past the cap and never draws under 96 tall.
4. **Footer** (optional). Full-bleed buttons; secondary (Default) left, Primary right; Close in the header dismisses, so no Cancel.

## Blanket

The Blanket is the full-screen layer inside the Modal template that holds the dimming fill and the Dialog above it, so the Dialog is placed, not drawn. It fills with the blanket token, pads 16 at the sides and 64 above and below, and centers the Dialog. It is part of this template, not exported on its own. The examples in this section follow the toolbar's Color mode switch.

<ComponentExample of={ModalStories.BlanketInline} title="Modal in a Blanket" description="A modal centered in the blanket at the 768 stand-in screen." />

<ComponentExample of={ModalStories.BlanketPads} title="Pads" description="Pad 16 at the sides and 64 above and below, shaded here; under 640 tall the vertical pad drops to 24." />

<ComponentExample of={ModalStories.BlanketScreenGrid} title="Screen grid" description="12 columns with a 16 gutter, centered and capped at 1232; a modal spans 4, 6, 8 or 10 of them." />

A Blanket interrupts the page for one task. It dims everything behind it with one fill, so the person sees only the layer on top. It never draws content of its own.

A task that keeps the page in view is a Panel, which floats with no Blanket. `inline` draws the Blanket and Dialog in the page flow at a stand-in screen of `screenWidth` and `screenHeight`, for docs and previews.

### Parts

1. **Blanket.** The screen-size layer with the blanket fill, the screen grid and the pads.
2. **Dialog.** The panel centered inside it with header, body and footer slots.

The Blanket carries the screen grid; page frames carry the content grid. Height, hug or fill, lives on the Dialog inside it.

### States

The Blanket is shown while the Modal is open and gone when it closes. `blanket` decides whether a press on the Blanket outside the Dialog closes the Modal (`true` by default; `static` or `false` keeps it open).

### Place a modal in the Blanket.

Let the Blanket place the modal. Do not detach it to move things around: the pads are the closest a modal gets to the screen edge.

Do: Center the Dialog on the Blanket with the built-in pads.

Don't: Detach or shift the panel past the Blanket edge pads.

### Blanket content guidelines

The Blanket holds no text of its own. The words belong to the Dialog it holds.

### Blanket accessibility

- The Blanket is a layout layer with no role. The Dialog inside it is the `dialog`.
- The blanket is a pointer shortcut, not a control. A keyboard closes a dialog from inside it, so the blanket takes no role and no key handler.
- The blanket at `--ds-blanket` keeps the dialog legible in Light and Dark. The toolbar's Contrast switch changes it with the rest of the page.
- A layer wider than a narrow screen keeps its width, and the Blanket scrolls.

## Usage

Use a Modal when the next step has to happen before anything else can: a confirm, a rare critical task with fewer than five fields, something to read before going on. It is always centered over a Blanket. A Panel that needs full attention becomes a Modal.

<DocsTable>
<thead>
<tr><th>Type</th><th>Use when</th><th>Footer</th></tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td>Passive</td><td>Something to read, with nothing to decide</td><td>None; Close only</td></tr>
<tr><td>Active</td><td>A choice, a confirm or a short form</td><td>Secondary (such as Discard or Keep) and the action; no Cancel</td></tr>
<tr><td>Acknowledge</td><td>One thing to read and accept</td><td>One button</td></tr>
<tr><td>Steps</td><td>A short ordered sequence</td><td>Back and Next</td></tr>
</tbody>
</DocsTable>

- For the dialog panel itself with no Blanket or modal behavior, use `Dialog` (`@/components`).
- For content read beside the page, use `Panel`.
- For a short aside off a control, use `Popup`.
- For a long or stepped task, use `Frame`.
- For a passing notice, use `Flag`.

## Best practices

### Open a Modal only from a person's action.

<RuleTag kind="review" />

A button, a link, an icon. Never on its own.

Do: Open a Modal when a person clicks an action or trigger.

Don't: Open a Modal automatically on page load.

### Size a confirm at the smallest step and step up with a detail line or a field.

<RuleTag kind="review" />

A confirm takes 4-col or 6-col; a short form is 8-col; a table, editor or steps is 10-col. Choose width by task.

<DoDontPair
    doExample={<ConfirmNarrow />}

    dontExample={<ConfirmWide />}
    dontCode={CODE.ConfirmWide}
    dontCaption="The same confirm at a wide step: empty width around one line, picked to make it look a certain way."

### Confirm inside the Modal, and never open a Modal on another Modal.

<RuleTag kind="automatic" />

Stacking holds the rule. The overlay module keeps a second Modal closed and calls its `onClose`.

<DoDontPair
    doExample={<ConfirmsInside />}
    doCode={CODE.ConfirmsInside}
    doCaption="One Modal that confirms inside itself, with a message and its own buttons."
    dontExample={<ModalOnModal />}
    dontCode={CODE.ModalOnModal}
    dontCaption="A Modal opened on another Modal, a second Blanket dimming the first."
/>

### Scroll the body between a fixed header and footer.

<RuleTag kind="automatic" />

Each width has a maximum height, the screen less `--blanket-pad-y` above and below. Header and footer are pinned and the footer never moves. Actions never sit in the body, which scrolls away; a missing-field error shows on the field and the body scrolls to it, and a rejected submit shows its message at the top of the body.

<DoDontPair
    doExample={<FooterPinnedModal />}

/>

### Use tabs for views, not steps.

<RuleTag kind="review" />

Two to five views of one thing opened in any order; one footer saves them all. Views in order are Steps, with Back and Next in the footer.

## Accessibility

- The Modal is a dialog named by its title, which is its h1; a Section inside opens at h2. A Modal with a custom header needs its own name.
- Focus moves into the Dialog on open (to the first input or control) and stays trapped until it closes, while the page behind stops scrolling. Escape, Close and the Blanket close it, and focus returns to what opened it.
- Set `blanket="static"` (or `false`) for an inline confirmation of a destructive action so a stray click outside does not abandon it.
- A body that scrolls is a tab stop. With tabs, Left and Right move between tabs and the panel is named by its tab.
- A blocked submit announces the first error; with reduced motion the color, icon, message and focus move carry it without the shake.
- Close is named "Close". The Danger button in a type-the-name confirm stays disabled until the name matches, and the caption carries the reason.
