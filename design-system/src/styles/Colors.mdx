<FoundationSection title="Overview">

Color in PLUS is a set of tokens, each named for the job it does: the text on a page, the edge of a field, the fill of the primary button. A token has one value in each color mode, so a component that uses the right token is right in light, dark and increased contrast without any work of its own.

```css
color: var(--ds-text-danger);
background: var(--ds-background-danger-subtler);
```

Figma holds them in `color / semantics`, with modes Light, Dark, Light IC and Dark IC; `color / primitives` stay hidden. Code reads them as `--ds-*` custom properties.

</FoundationSection>

<FoundationSection title="About color">

Color distinguishes PLUS and keeps the tutor and student apps consistent. A color is applied with a design token, so rather than choosing a shade you choose the token for the job: color, then the property (text, icon, border, background), then a role, an emphasis level and an interaction state.

Saturated colors carry meaning, highlight UI or tie related UI together. Neutral colors cover most backgrounds, text and shapes and carry no meaning of their own, though they can imply disabled. Alpha colors have varying transparency so UI adapts to the color or elevation under it.

</FoundationSection>

<FoundationSection title="How the tokens are built">

Two layers. Primitives are the raw ramps and stay hidden; never bind one. Semantic tokens point at a primitive and name a job, and they are the only color tokens you bind.

A name reads from the general to the specific: property, role, emphasis, state. In Figma the parts join with slashes, so `color/background/danger/bold/hovered`. In CSS, drop the leading `color` and join the parts with dashes behind `--ds-`, so `--ds-background-danger-bold-hovered`. That is the fill of a bold danger surface under the pointer.

Pick the family by job:

- A fill takes `--ds-background-<role>`, with `-subtler`, `-subtle` and `-bold` where the role defines them.
- A ground takes `--ds-surface`, `--ds-surface-sunken`, `--ds-surface-raised` or `--ds-surface-overlay`.
- Text takes `--ds-text-<role>`, and a glyph `--ds-icon-<role>`.
- An edge takes `--ds-border-<role>`; `--ds-border-<role>-subtle` is the pale edge of an outlined label, such as a Tag or a date Status.

</FoundationSection>

<FoundationSection title="Modes">

PLUS has two color modes, light and dark, and each has an increased contrast version for people who need stronger text and edges. That makes four modes. A fifth setting, auto, follows the operating system: `prefers-color-scheme` picks light or dark, and `prefers-contrast` turns increased contrast on.

- Dark is not light inverted. Raised and overlay surfaces get lighter as they rise, because a shadow is hard to see on a dark page.
- Increased contrast darkens text and meaningful borders in light and lightens them in dark. Text then reaches 7:1, and borders, icons and the focus ring reach 4.5:1. Tints, surfaces and the Backdrop stay as they are.
- Tokens map per theme. Design in dark mode by choosing the same token, and never map your own values.
- In code, the page sets `data-color-mode` (light, dark or auto) and `data-contrast-mode` (no-preference, more or auto). No attribute means light at standard contrast.
- In Figma, set the mode on the frame: Light, Dark, Light IC or Dark IC. Each swatch below shows the value the page resolves for its token right now, so switching the toolbar changes it at once.

<FoundationSection title="Roles">

Danger, warning, success, information and discovery carry meaning; brand marks the primary action. Each ships a subtle ground, a bold ground, and text, border and icon colors that pair with them. Pick the role by meaning, never by how the hue looks.

Danger, warning, success, information and discovery each come in four emphasis levels: the default tint for a Banner, subtler for a status label, subtle for a chip that is not interactive, and bold, the filled version. Discovery marks something new or proposed, such as a Suggestion.

Tokens: `color/background/<role>/subtler` or `bold`, `color/text/<role>`, `color/border/<role>` and `color/icon/<role>`. A neutral border that bounds a control takes `color/border/bold`; `color/border/default` is a divider. A bold warning ground is a light yellow, so its text is the dark `color/text/warning/inverse`.

A state steps along the token's own ladder. Hover and press have their own tokens; focus is a ring, never a fill, and [Border](?path=/docs/foundations-visual-border--docs) owns the ring. The colors that mark AI are on [AI UI › Color](?path=/docs/ai-ui-foundations-color--docs).

- A state takes the token that matches what the element is. A selected element takes `color/background/selected`, and `color/background/selected/hovered` when hovered.
- A row, menu item or sidebar item takes `color/background/neutral/subtle/hovered` and `/pressed`.
- A bold fill takes its own role's hovered and pressed steps, such as `color/background/brand/bold/hovered` or `color/background/danger/bold/pressed`.
- Only an avatar or an image, whose fill cannot change, keeps a translucent overlay: `color/interaction/hovered` and `color/interaction/pressed`.
- There are no icon hovered or pressed tokens. Show an icon's state with a subtle neutral background behind it.
- Keep selected and focused distinct. Selected persists after an interaction to show a current choice; focused shows where keyboard focus is. Use the selected tokens (background, text, icon, border) for selection and the focus ring only for focus. Selected stays blue, with a second cue such as a check or a border. Never use link, brand or focused tokens to show selection. When an element is both, keep the selected treatment and add the ring.
- Hovered and pressed are temporary: use the matching token for the element's current state, and keep a selected item recognizable while it is hovered or pressed.

```css
.my-element:hover {
  background-color: var(--ds-background-neutral-subtle-hovered);
}

.my-element:active {
  background-color: var(--ds-background-neutral-subtle-pressed);
}

.my-element.is-selected {
  background-color: var(--ds-background-selected);
}

```

<FoundationSubsection title="Field hover and focus">

Hover only hints that a field is interactive. Focus is the signal for keyboard input, and it is a ring around the field, never a change of fill. A field at hover takes `color/background/input/hovered` with the `color/border/input` edge; at focus it keeps its fill and gains the ring.

</FoundationSubsection>

</FoundationSection>

<FoundationSection title="Partner pairs and contrast">

Every foreground token has a ground it is made for, and the pair clears the WCAG bar in all four modes: 4.5:1 for text and 3:1 for icons, borders and the focus ring, rising to 7:1 and 4.5:1 under increased contrast. Use 3:1 for UI that is essential to understanding. Each ratio below is measured from the rendered colors.

- On a bold fill, text and icons take `color/text/inverse` and `color/icon/inverse`. Warning is the exception: `color/text/warning/inverse`, the dark ink.
- On a status tint, text takes the role's text token, or its bolder step on the subtler and subtle grounds.
- On any surface, text takes `color/text/default`, subtle or subtlest. Never carry meaning by color alone: a status has an icon or a word, and a chart has a legend or direct labels.

<FoundationSection title="Data-viz color">

Charts take the chart tokens, and every chart color does one job. Never reach for an accent hue or a role color to draw a series.

<ChartPalette />

- Use five or six categories at most and group the rest.
- Never mix categorical and status colors in one chart, and give every color a second cue: a label, a shape or a pattern.
- Titles, axes and grid lines wear text and border tokens, never a chart color.

In code, never name a chart color by hand: `chartPalette`, exported from `@/components`, says which token does which job. Color is the one foundation with chart rules of its own; a chart takes every other foundation (spacing, radius, elevation, stroke, type, layers, motion) from the same component tokens as the rest of the design system.

A single-series chart takes `--ds-chart-brand`, not slot 1 of categorical, and a de-emphasized comparison or reference line takes `--ds-chart-neutral`. Slot 1 of categorical is tuned to the brand chart hue for this reason: a one-series chart and the first series of a many-series chart read as the same color. The series identity stays put when a second series is added.

State tokens are for a value that means a state. The `-bold` step is the more extreme level of the same state: highest priority on `--ds-chart-danger-bold`, high on `--ds-chart-danger`. State colors are hard to tell apart with a color-vision deficiency, so a state always carries an icon, a label or a signed value too. The state tokens are success, warning, danger, information and discovery.

<FoundationSubsection title="Categorical order">

Assign series in this order, never skipping a slot.

<RefTable
    head={['Slot', 'Hue', 'Light', 'Dark', 'Light, increased contrast', 'Dark, increased contrast']}
    rows={[
        ['1 (brand)', 'lagoon', '600', '500', '850', '300'],
        ['2', 'orange', '800', '700', '1000', '500'],
        ['3', 'blue', '500', '500', '800', '300'],
        ['4', 'magenta', '800', '700', '1000', '500'],
        ['5', 'lime', '500', '400', '800', '250'],
        ['6', 'purple', '700', '700', '900', '500'],
        ['7', 'orange', '500', '500', '800', '300'],
        ['8', 'blue', '800', '700', '1000', '500'],
    ]}
/>

Three rules produced this order, and all three hold at once:

- **Brand leads.** Slot 1 is lagoon, the brand chart color's own hue, so a one-series chart and the first series of a six-series chart look like the same chart.
- **Neighbors alternate light and dark steps.** Slot 2 (orange-800, dark) sits between slot 1 (lagoon-600, lighter) and slot 3 (blue-500, lighter again), and the sequence keeps alternating through slot 6. That is what makes the order read in grayscale and for a color-blind viewer: two adjacent series on the same lightness would look identical once hue drops out.
- **Hues spread across the wheel.** No two adjacent slots share a hue family or sit beside each other on the wheel: lagoon, orange, blue, magenta, lime and purple are six stops spread wide.

Three hues stay out of the order, each for a reading reason: yellow reads as warning, red reads as danger, and teal sits too close to the brand hue. Lime and purple-500 double as the success and discovery state colors, so a chart uses categorical colors or state colors, never both.

Slots 7 and 8 reprise 2 (orange) and 3 (blue), for overflow past six series only. The sequence wraps back to slot 1 after 8 and repeats a color, so an eight-series chart is already past the point where it should have grouped its smallest categories into "Other".

Dark mode mostly steps one lighter than light mode: lagoon (1) 600 → 500, orange (2) 800 → 700, magenta (4) 800 → 700, lime (5) 500 → 400, blue (8) 800 → 700. The generated token carries the step, so no chart picks its own. Three slots hold their light step:

- Blue (3) and orange (7) hold at 500: a softer step would lose their place in the lightness alternation.
- Purple (6) keeps 700, because 600 crowded both of its dark-mode neighbors.

The mapping is by family and step, not by hex, so it is the same in every color plan.

</FoundationSubsection>

<FoundationSubsection title="Separators">

A separator, a gap or the `--ds-border-inverse` line between adjacent marks, marks where marks touch: an adjacent pair in a stacked bar, a histogram bin boundary, a waterfall step. The lightness alternation marks which series is which. Keep both: a viewer who cannot tell two near-touching hues apart at the seam needs the separator, and a viewer who cannot see hue at all needs the alternation even where two series never touch.

</FoundationSubsection>

<FoundationSubsection title="Sequential and diverging ramps">

Both ramps are built from the chart tokens.

- **Sequential** runs `--ds-background-accent-<hue>-subtler`, then `--ds-chart-<hue>-bold`, `-bolder` and `-boldest`. Each step sits further from the surface than the last, so the strongest tone is the biggest value: darker in light mode, lighter in dark.
- **Diverging** runs one hue's three chart emphases down to `--ds-background-accent-gray-subtler` at the midpoint, then the other hue's back up. The midpoint always sits nearer the surface than either arm's first step. The default pair is orange and blue; red with green is never a pair.
</FoundationSubsection>

<FoundationSubsection title="SMART subjects in a chart">

Each subject charts in its accent hue at the emphasis that keeps neighbors in S-M-A-R-T order apart: social-emotional orange (bold), mastering content teal (boldest), advocacy lime (bold), relationship magenta (boldest), technology tools brown (bold). Five subjects is near the categorical limit, so label them directly or keep the legend beside the chart.

</FoundationSubsection>

<FoundationSubsection title="Text, hover and chrome">

- A label sits beside its mark, so no foreground and background pair has to clear a contrast ratio. Where a label must sit on a fill anyway, such as a heatmap cell or a stacked segment, it takes `--ds-text` or `--ds-text-inverse`, whichever reads.
- A shape, a pattern, a direct label or a backing data table carries the same information the color carries, for every series and every state.
- A hovered mark takes its own `-hovered` token (`--ds-chart-categorical-1-hovered` and so on; every chart token has one). To bring one series forward, fade the others with `--ds-opacity-disabled`.
- Chart chrome (axis labels, gridlines, the tooltip, threshold lines) wears text and border tokens.
- Spacing and type inside a chart (label size, legend gaps, padding around a plot area) come from the same ladder and type tokens as every other component, so a chart carries no pixel value of its own.

</FoundationSubsection>

</FoundationSection>

<FoundationSection title="Chart order, targets and ranges">

Order and tone carry the reading before anyone hovers. Put the biggest first and the darkest first, and keep targets and ranges out of the bars.

- Sort categories from most to least. An ordinal set such as grades or tiers takes one hue ramp instead of separate hues.
- Darker means more: the strongest tone is the biggest value.
- A target is a green tick across the bar. A range is a green outline on the track. Do not shade bands or write values beside the bar: the numbers go in the hover tooltip.
- Text on a chart fill uses the partner token, `text/inverse` on a bold fill. Separate neighboring marks with a gap or an inverse border, never a second color.

<FoundationSection title="Usage">

Pick the pair, then the role, then the state.

<FoundationSubsection title="Pair text with its ground">

Take the text color from the same role as the ground. Gray text on a bold ground falls well below 4.5:1, in both modes.

<FoundationSection title="Disabled">

Disabled is a color, not an opacity. Fading a control with opacity makes it unreadable on some grounds and gives every control a different gray.

- Use `color/text/disabled`, `color/background/disabled` and `color/border/disabled`. They are the same in every control and keep their contrast pair in all four modes.
- The opacity token is for images only.
- Disabled still needs a reason a person can find: the session has ended, the form is incomplete. Say it in nearby text instead of leaving the control dead.

<FoundationSection title="Links">

A link takes `color/link/default`. In running text it is also underlined at rest, because the blue alone sits too close to body text.

- Menus, navigation, buttons and card titles may drop the underline: their position already says they are clickable.
- The link color has a pressed step but no hovered step. The underline stays on hover.
- Never use brand, selected or focused colors for a link.

<FoundationSection title="Accessibility">

- Every foreground token is made for a ground, and the pair clears the WCAG bar in all four modes: 4.5:1 for text and 3:1 for icons, borders and the focus ring, rising to 7:1 and 4.5:1 under increased contrast.
- Never carry meaning by color alone: a status has an icon or a word beside its color.
- Warning is the one intent whose edge and glyph differ from its fill: an icon takes `color/icon/warning` and an edge `color/border/warning`, because the yellow fill is 2.87:1 on the darkest surface.
- The WCAG AA bar: 3:1 for UI essential to understanding the experience and for text 24px or larger (1.4.11), 4.5:1 for text smaller than 24px (1.4.3).
- Pair a selected color with another cue, such as a check mark, underline, border or change in shape, so selection never relies on color alone.
- Check a new screen in all four modes before review: the toolbar's Color mode and Contrast switches set them here.

</FoundationSection>
