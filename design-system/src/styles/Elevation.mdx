<Title />

Elevation is how far a surface sits above the page. Each level is a pair of
tokens, a surface color and its shadow, and the two always travel together.
PLUS follows Atlassian's elevation model: a raised card or a menu keeps the
page's color and lifts by its shadow, and only the sunken frame changes fill.
The examples draw light and dark side by side, and follow the toolbar's
**Color mode** and **Contrast** switches.

### The levels

Five levels, lowest to highest. Pick the level by what the surface is, never
by how strong a shadow looks.

`--ds-surface-container` is not a level. It is a translucent fill for a
recessed area inside a surface, such as a summary strip in a card, so it works
on any surface beneath it.

Raised and overlay surfaces have `-hovered` and `-pressed` steps for a
surface that can be clicked, such as a clickable card.

### Cards

A card rests on the raised pair, `--ds-surface-raised` with
`--ds-shadow-raised`, and has no border. The raised shadow is a 1px ring at 5%
with a faint blur, so a resting card still reads almost flat in light. In dark,
the ring is light, so the card keeps an edge.

A clickable card lifts to `--ds-shadow-overlay` on hover and while it is
dragged. Pressed, it settles back to `--ds-shadow-raised` on
`--ds-surface-raised-pressed`. A card that cannot be clicked never lifts.

<div className="sb-ds-doc-section">
### Accessibility

- A shadow is never the only thing that tells two surfaces apart. Content on
  a card or a menu still has its own heading, and a modal sits on a scrim.
- Under increased contrast, the ring in the raised and overlay shadows darkens
  to a 3:1 line, so cards and menus keep a visible edge. In light increased
  contrast the frame turns white, and the Surface draws `--ds-border-surface`
  so its edge stays.
- Text on any of the five surfaces uses the text tokens from **Color**, which
  clear 4.5:1 on all of them.
</div>

<div className="sb-ds-doc-section">
### Legacy elevation names

`--elevation-light-1` to `-5` and the older `--surface-*` roles still resolve,
so components that have not moved keep rendering. Levels 1 and 2 point at
`--ds-shadow-raised`, and level 3 at `--ds-shadow-overlay`. Don't use them in
new work: use the pairs above.
</div>
