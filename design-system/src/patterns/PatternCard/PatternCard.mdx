A card is a raised container for one object on a page, such as a student, a session or a metric. It holds an optional image, a header over a body, and an optional footer, and it rests on the raised pair with no border. The pad and the radius follow the card size, and the stack between parts follows use.

The examples on this page follow the toolbar's Color mode switch.

## Usage

Use a card when one object needs its own container on the page. A card holds a heading, some content and, when people can act on it, a footer. This pattern is the shell: you fill its slots with your own parts.

Build a card in four steps. Pick the size by what it holds. Choose the header, or turn it off when the body carries its own title. Fill the body slot, spacing only inside the slot content with the Card stack tokens. Add the footer only for actions that commit.

### Size guidance

Content picks the size. Choose the smallest card that holds the content without clipping, then let the row decide how many fit. Height always hugs the content, and a card in a row stretches to the tallest card.

In Figma, `size` picks the pad, the radius and the minimum width together. In code, `padding` sets the pad and the radius, and `minWidth` sets the minimum width. A height cap is set with `style`'s `maxHeight`, and the card clips what passes it.

### Cards in a row

In a row, place the most cards that keep each card at its minimum width. Each row is the content width of its mode, with a 16 gutter. The count switches between viewport widths of 1224 and 1256 with the sidebar docked.

## Parts

1. **Image** (optional). A slot above everything else. It runs edge to edge, outside the pad, and the card radius clips its corners. Size the image you pass with `aspect-ratio`: 16:9, 4:3 or 1:1.
2. **Header.** The title, an optional description and an optional actions slot. A custom `header` replaces all three. In Figma the title truncates to one line and the description's `text` variant truncates at three lines or wraps; code leaves wrapping to CSS.
3. **Body.** A slot for text, a list or media.
4. **Footer** (optional). A left-aligned slot for actions that commit a change.

The image sits above a padded frame, and the header, body and footer sit inside it.

## Best practices

### Size the card to what it holds.

Visible empty space inside the card means size down. A clipped or crowded body means size up.

<DoDontPair
    doExample={<MetricInSmall />}
    doCaption="One number fits sm. The card is only as wide as it needs, so a row of them stays tidy."
    dontExample={<MetricInLarge />}
    dontCaption="The same number in lg leaves most of the card empty. Size down instead of filling the space."
/>

### Never put a card inside a card.

A card's raised fill and shadow are its own object boundary. Group related cards in a Section instead.

<DoDontPair
    doExample={<MetricsWithDividers />}
    doCaption="One card; three metrics stacked in its body, split by dividers."
    dontExample={<MetricsAsNestedCards />}
    dontCaption="Three mini cards inside the card. Borders and shadows pile up and the hierarchy flattens."
/>

### Keep the card in place while it loads.

<DoDontPair
    doExample={<LoadingInPlace />}
    doCaption="Loading keeps the card in place: a skeleton fills the body, so the layout does not jump when data arrives."
    dontExample={<LoadingLine />}
    dontCaption="A bare loading line takes the card away. The row collapses and everything below it moves."
/>

### Put commit actions in the footer.

A card that holds actions that commit a change gets a footer, and its actions start on the left, under the content. Only a modal footer aligns right.

## Accessibility

- The title is a heading one level below the section the card sits in: an h3 in a section, an h2 straight on a page. Nest the card in a Section rather than choosing a tag.
- A card that is clickable as a whole needs a role and a tab stop, and it must answer Enter and Space. When the card has one action, put a Button in the footer instead.
- Mark the card `aria-busy` while it loads. The skeleton bars are hidden from assistive technology.
- The hover lift is never the only cue that a card is clickable. Pair it with a visible action or a link in the card.
- Text sits on the raised surface in `--ds-text` and `--ds-text-subtle`, which meet contrast in every color mode.
- The card sets the current surface to its raised fill, so a sticky table cell inside it matches the card.
