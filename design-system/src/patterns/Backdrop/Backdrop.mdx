## Usage

A Backdrop interrupts the page for one task. It dims everything behind it with one fill, so the person sees only the layer on top. It never draws content of its own.

<DocsTable>
<thead>
<tr><th>Layer</th><th>When to use</th></tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td>Modal</td><td>One task blocks the page. The Backdrop centers it.</td></tr>
<tr><td>Edge layer</td><td>A full-height layer the Backdrop holds at an edge. A Panel is the way forward: it floats with no Backdrop.</td></tr>
</tbody>
</DocsTable>

The Backdrop draws the layer only. It does not trap focus, close on Escape or count toward the two-overlay limit. For a real dialog, use the core Modal component, which draws the same layers and backdrop token. `inline` draws the Backdrop in the page flow at a stand-in screen of `screenWidth` and `screenHeight`, for docs and previews.

## Parts

1. **Backdrop.** The screen-size layer with the backdrop fill, the screen grid and the pads.
2. **Layer.** The one modal it holds, centered. The Modal brings its body and footer slots.

The Backdrop carries the screen grid; page frames carry the content grid. Height, hug or fill, lives on the Modal inside it.

### States

A Backdrop has no states. It is shown while its layer is open and gone when it closes; a click on the backdrop, not on the layer, calls `onBackdropClick`.

## Best practices

A confirm Modal may open over a Panel, a Popup or a focused task, each with its own Backdrop. A Modal never opens on another Modal.

### Stack only a confirm over a Panel, a Popup or a focused task.

A confirm raised from a Panel or a focused task is a second Backdrop over the first. A Modal for another task waits until the first one closes.

<DoDontPair
    doExample={<EdgeLayerWithConfirm />}
    doCaption="An edge layer and the confirm it raises, stacked over it."
    dontExample={<EdgeLayerWithUnrelatedModal />}
    dontCaption="A modal for an unrelated task opened over an edge layer."
/>

### Never a Modal on a Modal.

A Modal confirms inside itself, with a warning SectionMessage and the danger button. The overlay module refuses a second Modal: it stays closed and calls its `onClose`.

<DoDontPair
    doExample={<ModalConfirmsInside />}
    doCaption="A Modal confirming inside itself, with a warning and the danger button."
    dontExample={<ModalOverModal />}
    dontCaption="A Modal opened on another Modal."
/>

### Confirm in a small modal.

<DoDontPair
    doExample={<ConfirmSmallDo />}
    doCaption="Confirm in a small modal."
    dontExample={<ConfirmSmallDont />}
    dontCaption="Confirm in a large modal."
/>

Popups, flags and tooltips never bring a backdrop. How a modal meets the Sidebar is on <a href="?path=/docs/templates-frame-frame--docs">Frame</a>.

### Place a modal in the Backdrop.

Let the Backdrop place the modal. Do not detach it to move things around: the pads are the closest a modal gets to the screen edge.

## Content guidelines

The Backdrop holds no text of its own. The words belong to the layer it holds.

<DocsTable>
<thead>
<tr><th>Do</th><th>Don't</th></tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td>Title a confirm with the question: "Discard changes?"</td><td>A second dialog that repeats the first one's title.</td></tr>
<tr><td>Name the confirm's primary action for what it does: "Discard changes".</td><td>"OK" or "Yes".</td></tr>
</tbody>

## Accessibility

- The Backdrop is a layout layer with no role. The Modal inside it is the dialog.
- The backdrop is a pointer shortcut, not a control. A keyboard closes a dialog from inside it, so the backdrop takes no role and no key handler.
- The backdrop at `--ds-blanket` keeps the dialog legible in Light and Dark. The toolbar's Contrast switch changes it with the rest of the page.
- A layer wider than a narrow screen keeps its width, and the Backdrop scrolls.
