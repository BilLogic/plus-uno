<Title>Surface</Title>
A surface is the rounded content box of a page: a tokenized container with a fill, an elevation, a pad and a radius, and a slot for your content. A level is a fill and its shadow together, so a surface never wears one without the other. It is a part of the surface container, and you can use it alone for any block that needs a level.

The examples on this page follow the **Color mode** switch in the toolbar. In dark, a level gets lighter as it rises.

### When to use it

Use a surface for a block that needs a fill at one of the four levels.

The surface container draws its own content box with the same fill and edge, so a page does not place a Surface inside it. For the page frame, use **Surface container**. For one object with a shape agreed, use **Card**, which is raised and ready to fill.

</div>

### Anatomy

1. **Surface** is the container. It carries the level's fill and shadow, the pad and the radius.
2. **Title** is optional. A surface that holds a page can carry the page's one h1 above the content.
3. **Content** is the slot. Sections go here.

In the surface container, the content box is a 12-column grid with a 16 gutter, capped at 1232 (reading 864) and never under 720. Sections stack 16, 24 and 40. The <a href="?path=/docs/foundations-patterns-frame--docs">surface container</a> page has the tokens.

#### Elevation levels

### Accessibility

- A surface has no role of its own. Use <code>as="main"</code> for the page's content box and <code>as="section"</code> for a labeled block.
- Pass <code>title</code> on the page's surface only. It renders the page's one h1, and Sections inside it open at h2.
- Text on a surface uses <code>--ds-text</code> at every level, in every color mode.
- In light increased contrast the default surface draws its edge ring, so it stays distinct from the frame behind it.
