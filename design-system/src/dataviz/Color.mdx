<FoundationSection title="Separators">

A separator — a gap, or a line between two touching marks — and the lightness alternation solve two different problems. The alternation says *which series this is*; the separator says *where one mark ends and the next begins*. A chart needs both: someone who can't tell two close tones apart at the seam still needs the separator, and someone who can't see hue at all still needs the alternation.

<FoundationSection title="Defaults and limits">

- **One color by default.** A single-series chart stays on the brand chart color — it does not borrow from the categorical order.
- **Five or six series, then group the rest.** Past that, fold the smallest categories into "Other" rather than reaching for slots 7 and 8.
- **Never color alone.** Every series or state also carries a shape, a pattern, a direct label, or a table a reader can check.
- **No text on a chart color.** A label sits beside its mark, never inside or on top of it.

</FoundationSection>
