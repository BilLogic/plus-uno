## Usage

Use Indicators when something needs a small label that says how it is. Ask what the label says: a number is a Count tag; a condition that can change is a Status tag; how much a number moved is a Change tag; a subject, a person or a category is a Tag. Skip an indicator when no action follows from it; past five or six states, use text.

## Best practices

### Never let color carry the meaning alone.

<RuleTag kind="review" />

The words say the state; the fill repeats it. A state that must be told apart at a glance adds a leading icon. A Change tag's arrow is decorative and its hidden text reads "Up 3 vs last week". A dot with no label is announced as nothing.

### Give each appearance one meaning.

<RuleTag kind="review" />

Neutral is not started, info is in progress, success is done, warning needs attention, danger failed or missed, discovery is new. Success never labels a problem. There is no color prop; a category with a color is a Tag.

<DoDontPair
    doExample={<StatusTag appearance="danger">Missed</StatusTag>}
    doCaption="Danger on a missed session: the word and the fill agree."
    dontExample={<StatusTag appearance="success">Missed</StatusTag>}
    dontCaption="Success on a missed session: the fill says good about bad news."
/>

### Show one status per object.

<RuleTag kind="review" />

Two statuses force people to decide which is true. A roll-up shows the worst child state.

### Keep a status to one or two words.

<RuleTag kind="review" />

A status is read at a glance; a sentence belongs in the row text. A Status tag truncates at 200 with its full text in a Tooltip.

### Keep most counts quiet and show nothing at zero.

<RuleTag kind="automatic" />

Subtle neutral is the default. Bold asks for action now, so at most one bold count per area. Pass the raw number: the component renders nothing at 0 and caps past `max` with a "+". No skeleton on a count; it appears when the number arrives.

### Set the Change tag's good direction so up can be bad.

<RuleTag kind="automatic" />

The sign gives the direction; the good direction gives the tone: up, down or none. Absences rising is danger; sessions held rising is success; a change with no judgment stays neutral. The value is always signed.

<DoDontPair
    doExample={<ChangeTag value={3} goodDirection="down" comparison="vs last week" />}
    doCaption="A rise in absences in danger: the good direction is down."
    dontExample={<ChangeTag value={3} goodDirection="up" comparison="vs last week" />}
    dontCaption="A rise in absences in success: the color says good about bad news."
/>

### Edit a status in the field, not on the label.

<RuleTag kind="review" />

A Status tag never opens a menu. Where a status can be changed, the field around it becomes an inline Select.

### Nest a count in a status instead of placing one beside it.

<RuleTag kind="review" />

"Needs review 3" reads as one label; two labels side by side read as unrelated.

## Accessibility

- A tag is read as its text; nothing suggests it can be pressed. A clipped tag becomes a tab stop so the Tooltip opens on focus.
- A count's meaning is in the label it sits on: "Inbox, 3 unread". A bare number announces nothing useful.
- A Change tag's arrow and value are hidden from assistive technology and its words read the direction and the comparison.
- A Status tag, Tag and Change tag keep an inside border, so a pale fill stays visible on any background and in forced colors.
