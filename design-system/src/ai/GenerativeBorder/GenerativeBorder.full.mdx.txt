## When to use

Use `GenerativeBorder` around the surface the agent is writing into, for exactly
as long as it is writing: a draft, a summary, a reply. Turn `active` on when
generation starts and off when it ends.

<DocsTable>
    <thead>
        <tr><th>Prop</th><th>Type</th><th>What it does</th></tr>
    </thead>
    <tbody>
        <tr><td><code>active</code></td><td>boolean</td><td>Shows the ring and sets <code>aria-busy</code> on the wrapper. Off, there is no ring.</td></tr>
        <tr><td><code>radius</code></td><td>card | card-sm | element | element-lg | full</td><td>The corner token the ring follows. Defaults to <code>card</code>.</td></tr>

        <tr><td><code>className</code></td><td>string</td><td>Added to the wrapper.</td></tr>

## Usage notes

<DoDont>

        </GenerativeBorder>
    </Dont>

### One generation indicator at a time.

Do not pair the ring with another eye-catching indicator, such as a spinner or a
shimmering skeleton, on the same surface. The ring is the signal.

### One ring per surface.

Do not nest two. A ring inside a ring reads as two generations and competes for
the same edge. Wrap the outer surface, or the one that is actually changing.

```jsx
// Incorrect: two rings on nested surfaces.
<GenerativeBorder active>
    <GenerativeBorder active>{draft}</GenerativeBorder>
</GenerativeBorder>

// Correct: the surface that is being written into.
<GenerativeBorder active={isGenerating}>{draft}</GenerativeBorder>
```

## Accessibility

- **The ring is decorative.** It is hidden from assistive technology.
- **`aria-busy` says what changed.** The wrapper is `aria-busy="true"` while
  `active`, which tells assistive technology the content is still changing.
- **Reduced motion.** Under `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` the ring does not
  turn. It is one solid `--ds-ai-border` line.
- **Contrast.** Both tones are AI color tokens that carry a value for every
  color and contrast mode. The ring never carries meaning alone.
